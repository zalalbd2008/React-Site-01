# National dental marketing agency study (Part 2, §3A)

**Internal research only.** Public pages must never name, quote, link or criticise any agency below. Do not reuse their wording, page structures, frameworks, diagrams or proprietary product names. Observations are paraphrased.

- **Observation date:** 2026-11-08
- **Companion files:** `research/competitors/serp-log.md` (query log), `research/competitors/national-agency-matrix.md` (pattern matrix and opportunities for Alpha Dental Media)

## 1. Method

1. **Queries.** Ran the 12 priority service queries through the WebSearch tool (US results, standard mode, one run each) and logged every returned URL with a result-type code in `serp-log.md`. The tool returns an **unordered list without ad, local-pack or AI-citation labels**, so no rankings or ad presence are claimed.
2. **Sampling.** From the query results plus well-known national dental/healthcare agencies that appeared in them, chose a practical sample of US agencies that (a) market dental services nationally, (b) were reachable, and (c) together cover single practices, specialty/implant practices, and DSOs/multi-location groups. Generalist multi-industry agencies were excluded unless dental/DSO was a primary positioning.
3. **Pages inspected per agency.** Homepage + one service page relevant to our S1–S9 services. Where the homepage made results claims, one case study was also opened; where possible one blog article was opened to check bylines and dates.
4. **Retrieval.** Pages were retrieved on 2026-11-08 with plain `curl` (no browser user-agent spoofing, no login, no form submission) and converted to text; structured-data dates (`datePublished` / `dateModified`) were read from page source. The WebFetch tool failed DNS resolution for every agency host this session, so it was not used.
5. **Recording.** For each agency: positioning, services, proof and whether it is evidenced (dates, baselines, time windows, definitions, permission statements), authorship/bylines, offer, CTA, pricing transparency, content strengths, and questions a practice owner would still have.
6. **What was not done.** No contact, no form submissions, no demos booked, no gated downloads, no traffic/spend/revenue estimates, no third-party traffic tools. Nothing below is an estimate of any agency's performance.

### Evidence grading used in the table

- **Evidenced (E):** result states a baseline, a dated time window, and what was counted.
- **Partly evidenced (P):** some of those (e.g. time window but no baseline; baseline but no date).
- **Unevidenced (U):** a headline number, percentage or testimonial with no baseline, date or definition.
- **Permission:** whether the page says the client consented to publication. None of the 13 agencies displayed an explicit consent/permission statement on the pages viewed (named clients and quotes imply but do not state it).

## 4. Content and article observations

- **Bylines:** of 10 agencies where an article was checked (A6's article URL returned 404; A12 and A13 not checked), **5 showed a named author** (A3, A5, A7, A9, A11), 1 used a generic company-employee byline (A2) and 4 showed a date but no visible author (A1, A4, A8, A10). Only **1** article (A9) showed both a published and a "last updated" date to readers. No article viewed named a reviewer.
- **Answer-first format:** one article (A9) opened with a labelled quick answer and a worked example; one AI-search service page (A11) answered buyer objections in FAQ form. Most service pages front-load benefits and push FAQs to the end.
- **Freshness signals:** structured-data `dateModified` values ranged from 2021 (A12 homepage) to the observation week (A9, A11, A4). Several agencies updated AI-search pages in Oct–Nov 2026.
- **AI search:** 8 of 13 sampled agencies list an AI-search/GEO/AEO service or mention it on the pages viewed (A1, A2, A3, A5, A6, A8, A9, A11); A10 and A12 market AI tools for other purposes (marketing operations, phones). Only A11 stated what is measured (citations, share of voice by prompt set, engines monitored) and that results cannot be promised; only A6 published a priced, time-boxed baseline/re-measure design.

## 5. Limitations

- **Small, purposive sample (13).** Chosen for coverage and reachability, not randomly; two prominent agencies (403) are missing. Findings describe these pages on this date, not the market.
- **Unordered search tool.** No rankings, ad presence, local-pack presence or AI Overview citations can be inferred (see `serp-log.md`).
- **Text extraction.** Pages were read as extracted text. Content rendered only by JavaScript, inside images, carousels, accordions or gated downloads may have been missed; e.g. pricing pages linked in navigation were not all opened (A4, A11), and A1's full case studies are downloads. "None published" means none on the pages viewed.
- **One service page per agency.** An agency may publish pricing, definitions or bylines elsewhere on its site.
- **No verification of claims.** Client results, awards, partner status and aggregate statistics were recorded as displayed and **not verified**. We made no estimates of any agency's traffic, ad spend, revenue or conversion rates, and used no third-party estimates.
- **Point in time.** Several pages were modified in the week before observation; offers with deadlines (A6, A12) will expire.
- **Internal use only.** Agency names appear here solely for research traceability.
