## Metro Atlanta's weight, and why the rest of Georgia still needs its own plans

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro covers 29 Georgia counties and had 6,482,182 people in July 2025, about 57% of the state. The next four markets covered on our Georgia pages are much smaller and structured differently.

Two patterns follow. First, outside Atlanta, the principal city is usually growing much more slowly than the counties around it, so an office's real catchment often extends beyond city limits. Second, Columbus is the exception in the other direction: city and metro both shrank, which shifts the emphasis toward retention and market share. Macon-Bibb County, which has its own metro and its own page, is also distinct from Atlanta.

## Consolidated governments and cross-state metros

Three of Georgia's markets are governed by consolidated or unified city-county governments, and the Census Bureau labels them accordingly:

- **Augusta:** "Augusta-Richmond County consolidated government (balance)". The county total is slightly larger than the balance because the balance excludes other incorporated places inside Richmond County.
- **Columbus:** the Columbus Consolidated Government, which describes itself as Georgia's first consolidated government. The city's population estimate equals Muscogee County's.
- **Athens:** the Athens-Clarke County Unified Government, reported by the Census Bureau as a "unified government (balance)".

<SourceNote ids={["ga1-census-pep-v2025-places", "columbus-consolidated-government-home", "accgov-home"]} />

For marketing, the practical rule is simple: patients search for Augusta, Columbus and Athens, so listings should use the mailing-address city consistently. The government structure matters when interpreting data, because "city" and "county" figures can look similar but are not identical.

Two metros cross state lines. The Census Bureau's Augusta metro includes Aiken and Edgefield counties in South Carolina, and its Columbus metro includes Russell County, Alabama. Google's local results weigh distance from the searcher, and ad location settings follow geography rather than state lines, so practices near the rivers should plan from patient ZIP codes. Advertising aimed at another state may raise that state's rules; we recommend counsel check them.

## Military installations as a patient-population consideration

Georgia's Army posts change the insurance conversation in three markets. Current names matter, because two changed in 2025:

- **Fort Benning**, just outside Columbus, was renamed Fort Moore in 2023 and restored as Fort Benning in April 2025. The Associated Press reported roughly 70,000 soldiers, civilian workers and family members stationed there.
- **Fort Gordon** in Augusta was Fort Eisenhower for about a year and a half and became Fort Gordon again in June 2025.
- **Fort Stewart–Hunter Army Airfield**: Hunter is in Savannah. The Army says 21,200 full-time soldiers, 4,350 civilian workers and contractors and more than 19,000 military retirees call Stewart-Hunter communities home.

<SourceNote ids={["army-fort-benning-home", "ap-fort-benning-name-restored-2025", "army-fort-gordon-home", "wrdw-fort-gordon-name-restored-2025", "army-fort-stewart-hunter-about"]} />

TRICARE separates dental coverage by beneficiary: active duty dental benefits for service members, the TRICARE Dental Program that family members can purchase, and FEDVIP dental plans for many retirees. A practice that states exactly which of these it participates in answers the question military households most need answered. In our website reviews in Columbus, Augusta and Savannah, no practice homepage did.

## University towns and dental education

Athens is Georgia's clearest university-town market. The University of Georgia reported 43,888 students in fall 2025, and Athens-Clarke's median age of 29.4 is the lowest of the five cities above. Enrollment is not the same as year-round residence; it is a reason to think about segments, turnover and the academic calendar, not a demand figure.

<SourceNote ids={["uga-fall-2025-enrollment", "census-acs5-2024-dp-athens-clarke-balance-ga"]} />

Augusta University describes its Dental College of Georgia as the state's only dental school, and in 2026 it broke ground on a Savannah campus slated to open in summer 2028. The university also states that Georgia ranks 46th nationally in dentists per capita and that 18 counties have no practicing dentist. These are the university's figures; for practices, they are most relevant to hiring and associate recruitment, and to the teaching clinics that some patients consider.

## Georgia's dental advertising rule

Georgia Board of Dentistry Rule 150-10-.01 defines advertising broadly and sets conditions any agency working for a Georgia practice should follow. This is a summary for planning, not legal advice; the practice's counsel should review promotions.

<Checklist title="Georgia advertising checks before an ad or page goes live" items={["The dentist has approved it: the rule makes the dentist responsible for ads placed by agents", "A copy is archived and kept for at least one year after last use", "Any fee range shows both the minimum and the maximum", "Advertised fees and free services stay available for 60 days after the ad last runs, unless another date is stated", "Related charges are covered or the required statement about additional charges is included", "No patient-identifiable information without written consent", "No superiority, painless or latest-equipment claims, and no quality-dentistry phrases", "Specialist wording only for Board-recognised specialties with the required education", "Location pages list only dentists who regularly treat patients at that location"]} />

<SourceNote ids={["ga-board-dentistry-rule-150-10-01"]} />

The location point matters for groups in particular: pages that imply a dentist practises at an office they rarely work in can fall within the rule's list of misleading statements.
