## What we found on Savannah websites

On 8 November 2026 we reviewed three Savannah-area dental practice homepages: a downtown general and cosmetic practice, a pediatric practice and a regional multi-location group. Several other sites blocked automated access or did not load and were skipped. We did not contact any practice.

- All three had tap-to-call numbers. One offered online scheduling; two used request forms.
- None advertised evening or weekend hours, and both sites that showed hours were closed or by appointment only on Fridays.
- Only the multi-location group showed offices in the growth areas outside the city.
- None mentioned military families, TRICARE or the TRICARE Dental Program on the homepage.
- Insurance information ranged from "most traditional plans, please call to verify" to a dental savings club.

## The business problems behind those observations

### Your catchment is moving, but your profile is fixed

A Business Profile represents one real location, and Google weighs distance from the searcher. A downtown or midtown office cannot rank everywhere new households are arriving. The practical question is whether people in the growth counties already drive to you. Patient ZIP codes answer it better than demographics.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-local-ranking", "google-gbp-guidelines"]} />

### Insurance answers are missing where they matter most

With public coverage at 37.9% in the city and a large military community, "please call to verify your insurance" pushes a deciding question onto the phone. That costs front-desk time and loses people who will not call.

### Four-day weeks and form-based booking

Friday closures and request forms are common in our small sample. Neither is a problem by itself. Both become one when ads run on Fridays, when callbacks take a day, or when profile hours are wrong.

## Dental SEO in Savannah

Dental SEO in Savannah means being the clearest, most trustworthy option for the parts of the metro you serve, not ranking across the coast. In practice:

- **Profile foundations.** One profile per real office, correct primary category, exact hours and holiday hours, and practitioner listings handled under Google's guidelines.
- **Service pages built around decisions.** For family, pediatric, implant or emergency care, explain who the service suits, what the first visit involves, costs policy, and insurance participation. Our article on [Google Maps visibility for dentists](/blog/google-maps-visibility-for-dentists/) explains why profile relevance and page content work together.
- **An insurance page that does real work.** List plan types you participate in. For military families, distinguish active duty dental benefits, the TRICARE Dental Program and FEDVIP rather than writing "we accept TRICARE".
- **Honest location content.** If patients come from Pooler or Richmond Hill, describe the route, parking and appointment availability. Do not publish pages for places you do not serve.
- **Prominence over time.** Google says reviews and links contribute to prominence. A steady, compliant review request process matters more than bursts.
