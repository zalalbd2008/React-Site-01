## How we work with Texas practices

Alpha Dental Media is a Houston-based dental marketing agency. Texas is one of our priority markets, alongside Florida, Georgia and Greater Los Angeles. We do not have offices or staff in other Texas cities: practices in Dallas, Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio and the state's other markets work with us remotely, through video calls, shared documents and read-only access to their Business Profiles, analytics and ad accounts. Houston practices work with us the same way.

<KeyAnswer>Texas is not one dental market. Two-thirds of Texans live in four metro areas that differ sharply in language, coverage, growth and commuting, and the fastest growth is at their outer edges. Good Texas dental marketing therefore starts metro by metro and office by office, within state advertising rules and the Medicaid and CHIP structure that shapes family dentistry.</KeyAnswer>

## The four metros that dominate Texas

Texas had about 31.7 million residents in the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate, 8.8% more than in April 2020. The four large metro areas — Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin and San Antonio — held about 21.8 million of them, roughly 69%, and accounted for about 88% of the state's population gain between 2024 and 2025 (our calculation from the same Census vintage).

<SourceNote ids={["census-v2025-counties-metros-release", "census-sub-est2025-texas", "omb-cbsa-delineation-2023"]} />

In 2024–25, Houston and Dallas-Fort Worth were the two fastest-growing U.S. metros by number of people added, Austin was sixth and San Antonio ninth. Five Texas counties — Harris, Collin, Montgomery, Fort Bend and Williamson — were in the national top ten for numeric growth, and the five fastest-growing U.S. cities of 20,000 or more were all Texan: four suburbs of Dallas-Fort Worth and Fulshear outside Houston. The Census Bureau noted that in large metros the fastest-growing counties tended to be on the outer edges, a pattern it called especially pronounced in Texas.

<SourceNote ids={["census-v2025-counties-metros-release", "census-v2025-cities-release"]} />

For dental practices, that pattern cuts two ways. Central-city practices often compete for share in slower-growing neighbourhoods, while suburban practices see new households and new competitors arrive at the same time.

**Houston** is the largest city in the state and the centre of a ten-county metro. Its metro has the longest mean commute of the four big metros, and the city is the most linguistically mixed of the state's five largest cities. Our [Houston page](/markets/texas/houston/) plans around intake capacity, language and drive-time corridors. Houston-area suburbs such as Katy and Sugar Land do not have pages yet; they will only get one if separate research justifies it.

**Dallas-Fort Worth** holds seven of our city pages, and they are planned separately because the data separates them. The Census Bureau splits the metro into two divisions. In the Dallas-Plano-Irving division, [Dallas](/markets/texas/dallas/) is a large core whose population was roughly flat in the latest estimate, so its page focuses on share, reputation and retention. Next door in Dallas County, [Irving](/markets/texas/irving/) is barely growing either, but most of its households rent and its residents are far more often foreign-born, which puts recall and language first. To the north in Collin County, [Plano](/markets/texas/plano/) is settled, highly insured and full of similar-looking offices; [Frisco](/markets/texas/frisco/) grew quickly but slowed sharply in 2024–25, so its page is about when to move from acquisition to retention; and [McKinney](/markets/texas/mckinney/) is still growing fast while the fastest growth moves into towns beyond it. In the Fort Worth-Arlington-Grapevine division, [Fort Worth](/markets/texas/fort-worth/) added more residents in 2024–25 than any U.S. city except Charlotte and has a larger share of children and homeowners than Dallas, so new-resident discovery leads; [Arlington](/markets/texas/arlington/), between the two big cities, is nearly flat, has a split payer mix and draws patients across city lines.

**Austin and Round Rock** share a metro but not a playbook. [Austin](/markets/texas/austin/) crossed one million residents but grew far more slowly than its suburbs, and its residents are more likely than those of the other large cities to have been born in another state, to work from home and to hold a degree; its page puts website conversion and content depth before paid traffic. [Round Rock](/markets/texas/round-rock/), in Williamson County, grew about 18% since 2020, and its main risk is the reverse: ad budgets that leak into central Austin, where patients rarely drive north for routine care.

**San Antonio** holds more than half its metro's population and has the highest share of Texas-born residents of the five largest cities. Our [San Antonio page](/markets/texas/san-antonio/) explains why reputation and bilingual trust come first.

**Three metros stand on their own.** [El Paso](/markets/texas/el-paso/) is a border metro where most residents speak Spanish at home and the population is roughly flat. [Corpus Christi](/markets/texas/corpus-christi/) is a coastal metro whose population has not changed since 2020 and whose practices must keep listings accurate through hurricane season. [Lubbock](/markets/texas/lubbock/) is a young university city with short commutes and a patient pool that renews every year. None of them behaves like a suburb of a bigger Texas metro, and advice written for Dallas or Houston transfers poorly.

## Medicaid and CHIP dental in Texas

Federal rules require states to provide dental benefits to children in Medicaid and CHIP; adult dental coverage is a state choice. In Texas, Texas Health Steps provides checkups and related services for children from birth through age 20 who have Medicaid, with dental checkups starting at six months. Most children's Medicaid dental benefits are administered as Children's Medicaid Dental Services by dental managed care organizations, with checkups recommended every three to six months.

<SourceNote ids={["medicaid-gov-dental-care", "tx-hhsc-texas-health-steps", "tx-hhsc-ths-dental-providers"]} />

HHSC's member comparison charts dated October 2026 list three plans for both children's Medicaid dental and CHIP dental: DentaQuest, MCNA Dental and UnitedHealthcare Dental. Nationally, the ADA Health Policy Institute reports that 41% of dentists participated in Medicaid or CHIP in 2024; that is a national figure, not a Texas one.

<SourceNote ids={["tx-hhsc-chip-dental-plans-chart", "tx-hhsc-medicaid-dental-plans-chart", "ada-hpi-medicaid-programs-2025"]} />

The marketing implication is practical. For pediatric and family practices, plan participation is one of the first facts parents check, and it varies by office. In the four or five practice websites we reviewed for each city page on 8 November 2026, statements ranged from naming Medicaid and CHIP explicitly, to saying the practice does not accept them, to saying nothing at all. Location pages, Business Profiles and ads should state participation precisely and be updated when contracts change. Our [pediatric dentists page](/who-we-help/pediatric-dentists/) covers the service mix in more depth.

## Texas advertising rules to check before you publish

The Texas State Board of Dental Examiners' professional-conduct rules include a subchapter on business promotion. Three provisions bear directly on marketing work. We read them on the Legal Information Institute's copy of the Texas Administrative Code, which is an unofficial mirror, so treat the checklist as items to check, not legal advice; the practice's counsel should confirm the current official text published by the Texas Secretary of State.

<Checklist title="Texas dental advertising checks" items={["Price advertising (22 TAC §108.53): state the service, related services usually required, that the fee is a minimum, the dates the price applies, and the standard fee behind any discount", "Never describe a service as free when a third party, including Medicaid, pays for it (§108.53)", "General dentists advertising specific services show General Dentist or General Dentistry directly after the dentist's name, in type at least as large as the service names (§108.55)", "Practice websites clearly disclose ownership of the site, services, office addresses and contact information, and licensure and qualifications of the dentists and providers (§108.59)", "Ads, landing pages and Business Profiles describe the same services, hours and offers", "Call recording is reviewed with counsel before it is enabled"]} />

<SourceNote ids={["tx-tac-108-53-fees", "tx-tac-108-55-general-dentists", "tx-tac-108-59-website-disclosures"]} />

These rules matter more where our website reviews found published new-patient prices and memberships to be common: three of five sampled homepages each in Dallas, Fort Worth and Austin, for example. On call tracking, the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press reports that Texas is a one-party-consent state for recording calls, but that is a secondary summary and does not settle privacy obligations; see [how we approach call tracking](/blog/dental-call-tracking/).

## What delivery from Houston looks like

Remote delivery does not change what the work needs from the practice. Every engagement starts with the [Dental Growth Audit](/dental-marketing-audit/), which reviews visibility, website conversion, advertising and measurement for each office. After that, the work depends on a few practice inputs: someone who can approve content and offers, accurate hours and plan participation for each office, phones answered during the hours being marketed, and monthly new-patient totals by office — totals only, never patient records.

We report on the same chain everywhere: inquiries, qualified inquiries, bookings, attended visits and, where the practice can share it, collected revenue. Texas does not change that chain, but the city table above suggests which link to check first in each market; the audit tests those hypotheses against each practice's own data. Definitions of each stage are on our [dental marketing metrics page](/resources/dental-marketing-metrics/).
