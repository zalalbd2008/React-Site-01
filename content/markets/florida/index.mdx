## How we work with Florida practices from Houston

We are based in Houston and do not have a Florida office, Florida staff or Florida clients to point to. Every service we offer for Florida practices — search visibility, Google Business Profile work, Google Ads, websites, reviews and measurement — is delivered remotely, with the practice's own accounts and data.

Remote delivery works when the practice supplies what a remote team cannot observe: real hours by day, the languages staff can actually serve, insurance networks and payment options, storm procedures, and:

- **Access:** manager access to the Google Business Profile (it has no view-only role), read access to Search Console, Analytics and Google Ads, and summary new-patient counts from the practice management system. Never patient records.

We start with the [Dental Growth Audit](/dental-marketing-audit/), which shows where inquiries are being lost before anything is spent on new campaigns.

### How the cities relate to each other

- **South Florida: one metro, three counties, four different plans.** [Miami](/markets/florida/miami/) is in Miami-Dade County, [Fort Lauderdale](/markets/florida/fort-lauderdale/) in Broward, and [West Palm Beach](/markets/florida/west-palm-beach/) and [Boca Raton](/markets/florida/boca-raton/) in Palm Beach County, which forms its own metropolitan division. Language at home falls and seasonal housing rises as you move north (table below), so Miami's page starts with bilingual intake, Fort Lauderdale's with the calendar, West Palm Beach's with measuring a three-way language mix and payer questions, and Boca Raton's with consultation-level evidence.
- **The Treasure Coast is the growth exception.** [Port St. Lucie](/markets/florida/port-st-lucie/) has its own metro area but sits in the same combined statistical area as Miami and Fort Lauderdale. Its 30.9% growth from April 2020 to July 2025 on Census Bureau estimates makes its page about capacity and commuters rather than language.
- **Tampa Bay is one metro split by water.** Hillsborough County grew 7.9% from 2020 to 2025 on BEBR estimates; Pinellas, home to St. Petersburg, grew 0.8% on BEBR's figures and fell 1.1% to July 2025 on the Census Bureau's. The [Tampa page](/markets/florida/tampa/) is about neighbourhood search on one side of the bay; the [St. Petersburg page](/markets/florida/st-petersburg/) is about reactivation and switchers in a county that has stopped growing.
- **The Gulf coast south of the bay is the oldest and most seasonal part of our coverage** (Collier County's seasonal-use share is 24.9%). [Naples](/markets/florida/naples/) is planned around two calendars and capacity; [Sarasota](/markets/florida/sarasota/) around the communities patients name and the short weeks we observed.
- **Central Florida's growth is uneven:** Osceola County grew 24.8%, Seminole 5.2%. The [Orlando page](/markets/florida/orlando/) is about being visible near that growth and bookable beyond a weekday-only week.
- **North Florida holds three very different cities.** [Jacksonville](/markets/florida/jacksonville/) is consolidated with Duval County across about 747 square miles, with the metro's fastest growth in St. Johns County (+27.4%); its page is about drive time. [Tallahassee](/markets/florida/tallahassee/) and [Gainesville](/markets/florida/gainesville/) are capital and university cities with median ages of 28.2 and 26.5 and fewer than 5% of workers employed outside their county, against 36.4% in Port St. Lucie. Tallahassee's page centres on insurance networks; Gainesville's on retaining permanent residents.

## What 52 practice websites across the 13 cities showed

On 8 November 2026 we reviewed 52 dental practice homepages across the 13 cities, three to five per city, using public pages only and submitting no forms. Pooled, three patterns stand out:

- **Language pages are rare everywhere.** 2 of the 52 sites had Spanish-language pages, one in Miami and one in Boca Raton; none of the four in Orlando did, although 28.0% of Orlando residents speak Spanish at home.
- **Part-year residents are not addressed.** None of the 16 sites in Naples, Sarasota, Fort Lauderdale and St. Petersburg had content for people who live in the area part of the year.
- **Weekend access is seldom visible.** 7 of the 52 showed any Saturday hours in their page content.

One site template and tag setup also recurred in the Orlando, Tampa and Jacksonville samples. These are small convenience samples; they describe websites, not how any practice performs, and each city page reports its own.

## Migration shapes how Florida patients look for a dentist

Florida grew by about 1.84 million people between April 2020 and April 2025, to 23,379,261, and BEBR attributes all of that growth to net migration: deaths exceeded births by roughly 101,000 over the period. Only 35.5% of Florida residents were born in the state, according to the ACS.

<SourceNote ids={["bebr-florida-estimates-2025", "census-acs5-2024-b05002-birthplace"]} />

The likely consequence is that many potential patients have no local dentist and no word-of-mouth network, so they search and compare. That puts weight on an accurate Business Profile (Google describes local results as based on relevance, distance and prominence; see our [Business Profile work](/services/dental-local-seo-google-business-profile/)), a booking path that works for someone with no prior relationship, and measurement that separates newcomers from existing patients.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-local-ranking"]} />

## Language at home differs more by region than anything else in the plan

Statewide, 30.6% of residents aged five and over speak a language other than English at home and 22.6% speak Spanish. At metro level the share runs from 55.2% around Miami to 13.8% around Jacksonville; at city level, from 77.2% in Miami to 9.3% in Naples.

<SourceNote ids={["census-acs5-2024-dp-florida-regions"]} />

In South Florida the ACS groups "French, Haitian, or Cajun" together; that group is 7.1% of Broward County residents five and over and 6.7% in Palm Beach County, and it does not isolate Haitian Creole. Language spoken at home also does not tell you which language a person prefers for booking or treatment discussions, so we treat it as a hypothesis to test with the practice's own intake data.

## Insurance and payment context

In the ACS, 11.5% of Floridians had no health insurance; dental coverage is not measured there, so this is context rather than a dental figure. The ADA Health Policy Institute classifies Florida's adult Medicaid dental benefit as emergency-only for 2021 through 2025, and reports that 25.6% of Florida dentists were enrolled as Medicaid or CHIP providers in 2024. Among Florida children continuously enrolled in Medicaid or CHIP, 40.7% visited a dentist in 2024; among privately insured children, 63.6% did in 2023.

<SourceNote ids={["census-acs5-2024-dp-florida-regions", "ada-hpi-medicaid-tables-2025"]} />

These are state figures and never describe a particular city. Pediatric practices accepting Medicaid and fee-for-service cosmetic practices face very different pictures, and both need pages that say plainly what is accepted.

## Florida rules that shape dental advertising

Florida regulates dental advertising more specifically than many states, and its statutory definition of advertising includes websites and social media. The points that most often affect marketing copy:

- **Section 466.019, Florida Statutes**, bars false, misleading or deceptive statements in a dentist's advertising, including laudatory statements about the dentist, statements comparing the quality of dental services with other services, claims likely to create unjustified expectations of favorable results, statements likely to appeal primarily to a layperson's fears, and fee information without a disclaimer that it is a minimum fee only. Advertising certain telehealth services requires a specified disclaimer.
- **Section 456.062** requires a specific statement, in capital letters clearly distinguishable from the rest of the text, in any advertisement for a free, discounted-fee or reduced-fee service, examination or treatment.
- **Rule 64B5-4.002, Florida Administrative Code**, requires all advertising to identify the Florida-licensed dentist who assumes total responsibility for it, prohibits in-person and telephone solicitation by a dentist or agent (scheduling and follow-up with patients excepted), and bars naming people who neither practise at the advertised location nor own the practice. The official rule page could not be retrieved on 8 November 2026, so these points were checked against a reproduction of the rule text; confirm the current official version before relying on any detail.

## Hurricane season as a communications and continuity issue

NOAA's National Hurricane Center gives the Atlantic hurricane season as June 1 to November 30, with a climatological peak around 10 September and most activity between mid-August and mid-October. The marketing question is whether patients get accurate information when the office closes, and whether paid campaigns keep sending people to a closed office.

<SourceNote ids={["noaa-nhc-hurricane-climatology"]} />

Google's guidance draws a line that matters here: special hours are for temporary changes or closures of up to six days in a row; for seven days or more, or an unknown period, Google says to mark the business temporarily closed. Google Ads location assets stop showing for locations marked temporarily or permanently closed in Business Profile.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-special-hours", "google-ads-location-assets"]} />
