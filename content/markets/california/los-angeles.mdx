## Who we serve in the City of Los Angeles

We work with practices and groups whose offices are inside the **City of Los Angeles**, from family and pediatric offices to implant and orthodontic practices. We do this remotely from Houston, with no Los Angeles office, so the one thing we cannot research from a distance is the most useful: a totals-only count of your new patients by ZIP code, which tells us which part of this very large city is really your market.

It helps to be exact about the market. The City of Los Angeles is one incorporated city in **Los Angeles County**, and both sit inside the **Los Angeles–Long Beach–Anaheim, CA Metro Area**. The county has 88 incorporated cities, so many places people casually call "Los Angeles", such as [Santa Monica](/markets/california/santa-monica/), [Beverly Hills](/markets/california/beverly-hills/), Culver City, [Burbank](/markets/california/burbank/) or [Glendale](/markets/california/glendale/), are separate cities with their own addresses. Koreatown, Silver Lake, Echo Park, Westwood, Hollywood and Valley neighbourhoods such as Van Nuys and North Hollywood are inside the city.

<SourceNote ids={["lacounty-about"]} />

The city alone is a very large market. ACS 2020–2024 estimates put its population at 3,857,263 in 1,439,097 households, about 39% of the county's residents, spread across roughly 470 square miles. The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimates put the city at 3,869,089 in July 2025, about 0.8% below its April 2020 base: the smallest decline of the Los Angeles County cities on this site, against 3.2% for the county. Population is roughly flat rather than growing, so a practice's growth is more likely to come from competing well in its own neighbourhoods than from new households arriving.

## What our Los Angeles research found

On 8 November 2026 we ran several web searches for dentists in Los Angeles and reviewed four practice websites inside the city: two independent practices on the east side, one independent group on the Wilshire corridor and one DSO-branded office page in Mid-City. We also opened four dental marketing agency pages that target "Los Angeles". This is a small, non-random sample, so we treat it as a set of prompts for an audit rather than as market statistics.

- **Directories crowd the broad searches.** Searches for family dentists in Los Angeles returned mostly directories, insurance marketplaces and listing aggregators rather than practice websites.
- **Booking was available everywhere, in different forms.** All four sites had a tap-to-call number and a booking or appointment-request path; two used an identifiable online scheduling tool.
- **Payment information varied sharply.** One site listed dental HMO plans and Denti-Cal; another stated it accepts neither dental HMO plans nor Medi-Cal; two said only that they work with PPO plans.
- **Language support was uneven.** Two of the four offered Spanish: one with a full Spanish version of the site, one with a single "se habla español" line.
- **Hours differed.** Two published Saturday hours, one offered Saturdays by appointment, and one stayed open until 7 pm on a weekday.
- **Agency pages blurred city and metro.** Three of the four agency pages we read described "Los Angeles" using places that are separate cities.

## The business challenges specific to Los Angeles

### Scale makes "Los Angeles" the wrong unit

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and popularity. Distance is the factor a practice cannot change, and in a 470-square-mile city it dominates. ACS puts the city's mean travel time to work at 30.7 minutes, a reminder that crossing town is a real cost. A Westwood practice and a Boyle Heights practice are effectively in different markets, with different competitors, languages and payment mixes. We start every Los Angeles engagement by drawing the catchment from your patient ZIP codes, not from the city boundary.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-local-ranking", "acs-2024-dp03-los-angeles-city"]} />

### Languages differ by neighbourhood, and staff capacity matters more than demographics

In the city, 56.4% of residents aged 5 and over speak a language other than English at home and 25.0% speak English less than "very well". Spanish is the largest group at 39.3%, followed by the Census grouping of other Indo-European languages (5.3%), Tagalog (2.3%), Korean (2.3%) and Chinese (1.7%). The DHCS Medi-Cal Dental provider listing points the same way: after Spanish, the non-English languages most often listed by providers with a Los Angeles billing city were Korean, Farsi, Tagalog, Russian and Armenian.

<SourceNote ids={["acs-2024-dp02-los-angeles-city", "acs-2024-c16001-los-angeles-city", "chhs-dhcs-medi-cal-dental-ffs-providers"]} />

These figures describe residents, not the preferences of any patient. They tell us which languages to ask about, not which pages to build. The test is whether your team answers the phone in that language, on which days, and whether patients who call in it go on to book.

### A wide spread of payment situations

The city's ACS figures show 9.5% of residents without health insurance and 42.9% with some public coverage, while 36.0% of occupied homes are owner-occupied. Those are medical-coverage and housing measures, not dental-benefit data, but together they suggest a market where the same search can come from a PPO patient, a Medi-Cal member or a self-pay patient comparing membership plans. Your site needs to tell each of them, quickly, whether you are an option.

## Recommended channel mix for a Los Angeles practice, and why

Our default order for a single-office practice inside the city is different from what we would recommend for a smaller city, because of the scale and directory competition described above.

1. **Business Profile and neighbourhood visibility first.** Because distance shapes map results, the profile is where nearby patients find you. We check categories, hours, services, photos and review handling, and fix listing inconsistencies. The [local SEO and Business Profile service](/services/dental-local-seo-google-business-profile/) covers the method, and our guide to [why practices do not appear in Google Maps](/blog/google-maps-visibility-for-dentists/) explains the diagnosis.
2. **Website conversion second.** Every visit from a nearby searcher should find hours, payment information, language support and a booking path within a screen or two on a phone.
3. **Search ads third, tightly bounded.** Google Ads offers a Presence setting that reaches people in your targeted locations rather than people elsewhere who have shown interest in them. Google recommends broader targeting by default, but says Presence makes sense when you only want to reach users in specific locations. In a city this large the risk is not visitors but distance inside the city itself, so we would normally pair Presence with radius or ZIP targeting drawn from your catchment, and set a different radius per service, so budget is not spent on searchers an hour away. The [Google Ads management service](/services/dental-google-ads-management/) explains how we structure and measure campaigns.
4. **Organic content for services patients travel for.** Implants, orthodontics, sedation and specialist care can justify a wider catchment, so these pages target a larger area than hygiene or family dentistry.

## Dental SEO in Los Angeles

Dental SEO in Los Angeles is mainly a local, neighbourhood-level problem rather than a city-wide ranking contest. The practical goal is to be the clearest and most accurate option, with steady genuine reviews, for specific services near your office, then to extend reach for services patients will travel for.

Within that, the work has four parts.

- **Entity accuracy.** Your name, address, phone number, hours and city must agree on your site, your Business Profile, structured data and the major directories. In a county with 88 cities, an address in one city and content claiming another creates confusion for search engines and patients. If you are inside the City of Los Angeles, it is fine to name your neighbourhood; just do not invent locations you do not have.
- **Neighbourhood relevance without doorway pages.** One strong location page that describes how patients reach you, parking, nearby landmarks, languages and hours is more useful than a dozen thin pages for neighbourhoods you do not serve. Additional area pages are justified only when patient data shows real demand and you have something specific to say.
- **Service depth.** Separate, substantive pages for the services you want more of, with cost and payment explanation, what a first visit involves and who provides the care. The [dental SEO service](/services/dental-seo-company/) explains how we plan and measure this.
- **Language versions done properly.** Google recommends separate URLs for each language version rather than switching language with cookies or browser settings. A Spanish or Korean page should be a real page for patients, linked to its English equivalent, not an automatic translation.

We treat search terms as research candidates until your own Search Console data shows which neighbourhood and service combinations bring impressions, clicks and calls. We do not promise rankings, and we report on booked and attended visits as well as visibility.

## What the website must answer for Los Angeles patients

For a Los Angeles practice, the website has to answer three questions fast on a phone: can I get there, can I pay, and can I be understood. That translates into a short list of checks, which the [dental website audit checklist](/resources/dental-website-audit-checklist/) covers in full:

- Hours, address and parking or transit notes visible without scrolling far, matching the Business Profile exactly.
- An accepted and not-accepted plans block on every service page, including dental HMO and Medi-Cal status.
- One clear booking path on mobile, with the call button and booking option both visible.
- Language support stated precisely, for example which days a Spanish-speaking coordinator is in, rather than a general claim.
- Current reviews and testimonials used with permission, and never patient details.
