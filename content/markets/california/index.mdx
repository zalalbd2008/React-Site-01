## What "California" means for Alpha Dental Media

Our California work covers **Greater Los Angeles**: dental practices, dental groups and DSOs with offices in the incorporated cities of **Los Angeles County**. Los Angeles County sits inside the **Los Angeles–Long Beach–Anaheim, CA Metro Area**, which also includes Orange County. We are a Houston-based agency, we serve these practices remotely, and we do not have a California office or California staff.

We are deliberately not extending the local program to the rest of the state. San Diego, the Bay Area, Sacramento and the Central Valley are different markets with different search behaviour, competitors and patient flows, and a page that pretended otherwise would be generic. Concentrating on one county lets each city page rest on that city's own data and observations rather than on assumptions borrowed from elsewhere.

## How Los Angeles County is put together, and why city names matter

Los Angeles County is not one city with suburbs. The county describes itself as covering 4,084 square miles with nearly 10 million residents, and as having grown from 76 to 88 incorporated cities, alongside large unincorporated areas.

<SourceNote ids={["lacounty-about"]} />

That structure shapes dental marketing in three practical ways:

1. **Search entities are city-specific.** Glendale, Pasadena, Burbank, Santa Monica and Long Beach are separate cities, not neighbourhoods of Los Angeles. Google Business Profile guidelines ask that a profile reflect the business's real-world name and address, so the city in your address, your site's location content and your structured data should agree with each other.
2. **"Los Angeles" is ambiguous.** People use it for a city, a county and a metro area. In the agency pages we reviewed on 8 November 2026, "Los Angeles" was often used to cover places that are separate cities. For a practice, the useful question is narrower: which neighbourhoods and nearby cities do your patients actually come from?
3. **Catchments cross city lines.** A Pasadena or Glendale office is likely to draw from neighbouring cities and unincorporated communities; a Long Beach office may draw from across the Orange County line. These are hypotheses we test with your patient ZIP codes, not assumptions we build campaigns on.

The four city pages take these differences in different directions:

- **[The City of Los Angeles](/markets/california/los-angeles/)** is a problem of scale and fragmentation: 3.86 million residents across 470 square miles, where neighbourhood-level relevance matters more than the city name.
- **[Long Beach](/markets/california/long-beach/)** is the densest of the four and has the youngest age profile; in our sample, price-led new-patient offers and messy phone numbers were the notable patterns.
- **[Pasadena](/markets/california/pasadena/)** is small in area, so a practice's market almost certainly extends into neighbouring cities; the sites we reviewed offered no language options despite 42.6% of residents speaking a language other than English at home.
- **[Glendale](/markets/california/glendale/)** is where language is most central: two-thirds of residents aged 5 and over speak a language other than English at home, and about half of residents were born outside the United States.

Together these four cities account for about 4.6 million of Los Angeles County's 9.8 million residents. Population is also not growing: ACS period estimates for the county were about 2.5% lower in 2019–2023 than in 2014–2018, while the state as a whole was roughly flat. A flat or shrinking population means growth tends to come from winning patients from other practices and keeping existing ones, rather than from new households arriving.

## Medi-Cal Dental and what it means for marketing decisions

Medi-Cal, California's Medicaid program, includes dental benefits administered by the Department of Health Care Services (DHCS). For marketing, the relevance is not clinical. It is about **who your messaging invites** and whether your phone team and schedule are set up for them.

DHCS publishes county-level utilization data. Summing its age bands for Los Angeles County in calendar year 2024, about 3.5 million members were continuously enrolled for the year, and 39.5% of them had at least one dental service or safety-net-clinic dental encounter. The rate was 58.0% for members aged 0 to 20 and 30.2% for those aged 21 and over.

<SourceNote ids={["chhs-dhcs-medi-cal-dental-measures-county"]} />

Those figures describe a county population, not demand for any one office, but they explain why payment-path clarity matters so much here. In our website samples, some practices listed Denti-Cal or Medi-Cal among accepted plans, one stated plainly that it does not accept Medi-Cal or dental HMO plans, and others said only "PPO". Each of those can be the right business choice. The marketing mistake is ambiguity, which sends patients to the phone to ask a question the website could have answered.

At county level, ACS figures add context: 40.2% of Los Angeles County residents have some form of public health coverage and 8.7% have none. Public coverage there means medical coverage such as Medi-Cal or Medicare, so it is a reason to make payment information clear, not a measure of dental benefits.

## Language diversity across the county

Los Angeles County is linguistically diverse at a scale few markets match. Among residents aged 5 and over, 55.1% speak a language other than English at home, compared with 44.1% statewide, and 23.2% speak English less than "very well". Spanish is the largest group at 37.7%; Chinese languages and the Census grouping "other Indo-European languages" are each about 4.1%, followed by Tagalog and Korean.

<SourceNote ids={["acs-2023-dp02-la-county", "acs-2023-dp02-california", "acs-2023-c16001-la-county"]} />

We treat these numbers as a reason to ask questions, not as a targeting plan. Speaking a language at home does not mean a patient wants to search, book or be treated in it. The decisions that matter are practical: which languages your team can support on the phone and in the chair, on which days, and whether translated pages say something useful rather than repeating a menu of services. The DHCS Medi-Cal Dental provider listing is a reminder that this varies city by city: the most frequently listed non-English language among providers was Spanish for Los Angeles, Long Beach and Pasadena billing addresses, but Armenian for Glendale.

## California privacy law and your practice website

The **California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)**, as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act, is a consideration for practice websites in a way that has no direct equivalent in Texas, Florida or Georgia. According to the California Attorney General, it applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California and meet at least one threshold: annual gross revenue above a set amount, buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more California residents or households, or deriving half or more of annual revenue from selling personal information. The California Privacy Protection Agency lists the revenue threshold as $26.625 million effective 1 January 2025. The law exempts certain medical information.

<SourceNote ids={["ca-oag-ccpa", "cppa-ccpa-faq"]} />

What that means in practice is unclear for many dental businesses, and we do not make compliance judgements. A single-office practice may be below every threshold; a larger group or DSO may not be. Even where most patient data is exempt medical information, the marketing layer is where questions tend to arise: advertising pixels, analytics tags and session-recording tools can send visitor data to third parties. The Attorney General's page also notes that covered businesses must honour a browser-level opt-out signal such as Global Privacy Control.

## Call recording and call tracking

Call tracking is one of the most useful measurement tools for a dental practice, because so many new patients still phone. Many call-tracking platforms also offer call recording, and California treats recording differently from many states. The Legislative Counsel's digest of a 2015 bill summarised existing law as making it a crime to record a confidential communication, including a telephone call, without the consent of all parties.

<SourceNote ids={["ca-ab925-2015-digest-pc632"]} />

That digest is a summary from 2015, not the current text of Penal Code 632, so treat it as a reason to check, not as legal guidance. Our approach is to measure calls without recording by default: tracking numbers, call duration, missed-call rates and a simple outcome tag entered by your team. Recording is enabled only after your counsel approves the disclosure, and recordings are never used in reports that include patient details. The [dental call tracking guide](/blog/dental-call-tracking/) explains how to count calls without treating every one as a new patient.
