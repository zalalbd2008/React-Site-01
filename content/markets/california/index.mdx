## What "California" means for Alpha Dental Media

Our California work covers **Greater Los Angeles only**: dental practices, dental groups and DSOs with offices in the incorporated cities of **Los Angeles County**. Los Angeles County sits inside the **Los Angeles–Long Beach–Anaheim, CA Metro Area**, which also includes Orange County. We are a Houston-based agency, we serve these practices remotely, and we have no California office or California staff.

We are deliberately not extending the local program to the rest of the state. San Diego, the Bay Area, Sacramento and the Central Valley are different markets, and concentrating on one county lets each city page rest on that city's own data rather than on assumptions borrowed from elsewhere.

## How Los Angeles County is put together, and why city names matter

Los Angeles County is not one city with suburbs. The county describes itself as covering 4,084 square miles with nearly 10 million residents, and as having grown from 76 to 88 incorporated cities, alongside large unincorporated areas.

<SourceNote ids={["lacounty-about"]} />

That structure shapes dental marketing in three practical ways:

1. **Search entities are city-specific.** Glendale, Pasadena, Burbank, Santa Monica and Long Beach are separate cities, not neighbourhoods of Los Angeles. Google Business Profile guidelines ask that a profile reflect the business's real-world name and address, so the city in your address, your site's location content and your structured data should agree with each other.
2. **"Los Angeles" is ambiguous.** People use it for a city, a county and a metro area. In the agency pages we reviewed on 8 November 2026, "Los Angeles" was often used to cover places that are separate cities. For a practice, the useful question is narrower: which neighbourhoods and nearby cities do your patients actually come from?
3. **Catchments cross city lines.** A Burbank or Glendale office is likely to draw from neighbouring cities and City of Los Angeles neighbourhoods; a Long Beach office may draw from across the Orange County line. These are hypotheses we test with your patient ZIP codes, not assumptions we build campaigns on.

### How the cities relate to each other

The cities fall into clusters whose practices may share patients.

- **The Westside.** [Santa Monica](/markets/california/santa-monica/) and [Beverly Hills](/markets/california/beverly-hills/) are separate cities almost surrounded by [City of Los Angeles](/markets/california/los-angeles/) neighbourhoods such as Brentwood, Venice and Century City. All three pages deal with boundaries, but differently: the City of Los Angeles page narrows a huge city to neighbourhoods, Santa Monica deals with density and renter turnover, and Beverly Hills with a small resident base, a contested name and referral-led specialists.
- **North and northeast of downtown.** [Glendale](/markets/california/glendale/), [Burbank](/markets/california/burbank/) and [Pasadena](/markets/california/pasadena/) border each other or nearby Los Angeles neighbourhoods. Glendale's plan is led by language, Burbank's by trade area, and Pasadena's by elective consult quality and booking attribution.
- **The South Bay and harbour.** [Long Beach](/markets/california/long-beach/) and [Torrance](/markets/california/torrance/) are near each other but face different problems: phone and offer discipline in a dense city, and retention in an older, shrinking one.
- **The southeast and the east San Gabriel Valley.** [Downey](/markets/california/downey/) is a bilingual Spanish-and-English market where payment clarity decides many calls; [West Covina](/markets/california/west-covina/) has several large language communities and, in our sample, a national implant-centre brand with an office in the city.
- **The north county.** [Santa Clarita](/markets/california/santa-clarita/) is the most spread out, with the longest average commute of the eleven and the highest share of owner-occupied homes, so community-level visibility comes first.

### Three patterns that hold across the county

First, **every one of the eleven cities had fewer residents in July 2025 than in April 2020** in the Census Bureau's estimates, from 0.8% fewer in the City of Los Angeles to 5.9% fewer in Beverly Hills; the county fell 3.2% while California as a whole fell 0.5%. Growth for a practice is therefore more likely to come from winning and keeping patients than from new households arriving, which is why retention appears in several city plans.

Second, **language varies more between neighbouring cities than averages suggest.** The share of residents aged five and over who speak a language other than English at home ranges from 27.8% in Santa Monica to 66.2% in Glendale, and the largest groups differ: Spanish in Downey, a broad "other Indo-European" category in Glendale, several Asian languages in West Covina and Torrance.

Third, **the practice websites we sampled rarely matched that variety.** Across 38 practice home pages reviewed in the eleven cities on 8 November 2026, three to four per city, four offered Spanish pages or a Spanish-language option, none offered pages in any other non-English language, several more listed staff languages in a single line, and one used an automatic translation widget. The samples are small and not representative, so we read this as a set of questions for an audit, not as a market finding.

## Medi-Cal Dental and what it means for marketing decisions

Medi-Cal, California's Medicaid program, includes dental benefits administered by the Department of Health Care Services (DHCS). For marketing, the relevance is not clinical. It is about **who your messaging invites** and whether your phone team and schedule are set up for them.

DHCS publishes county-level utilization data. Summing its age bands for Los Angeles County in calendar year 2024, about 3.5 million members were continuously enrolled for the year, and 39.5% of them had at least one dental service or safety-net-clinic dental encounter. The rate was 58.0% for members aged 0 to 20 and 30.2% for those aged 21 and over.

<SourceNote ids={["chhs-dhcs-medi-cal-dental-measures-county"]} />

Those figures describe a county population, not demand for any one office, but they explain why payment-path clarity matters. In our website samples, some practices listed Denti-Cal or Medi-Cal among accepted plans, one stated plainly that it accepts neither Medi-Cal nor dental HMO plans, and others said only "PPO". Each can be the right business choice; the marketing mistake is ambiguity, which sends patients to the phone to ask.

At county level, ACS figures add context: 41.2% of Los Angeles County residents have some form of public health coverage and 8.2% have none. Public coverage there means medical coverage such as Medi-Cal or Medicare, so it is a reason to make payment information clear, not a measure of dental benefits.

<SourceNote ids={["acs-2024-dp03-la-county", "obs-ca1-practices-los-angeles", "obs-ca1-practices-long-beach"]} />

## Language diversity: a question to ask, not a targeting plan

Among Los Angeles County residents aged 5 and over, 55.1% speak a language other than English at home, compared with 44.4% statewide, and 23.4% speak English less than "very well". Spanish is the largest group at 37.6%; Chinese languages and the Census grouping "other Indo-European languages" are each about 4.1%, followed by Tagalog and Korean.

<SourceNote ids={["acs-2024-dp02-la-county", "acs-2024-dp02-california", "acs-2024-c16001-la-county"]} />

Speaking a language at home does not mean a patient wants to search, book or be treated in it, so these numbers never decide a plan on their own. The decisions that matter are practical: which languages your team can support on the phone and in the chair, on which days, and whether translated pages say something useful rather than repeating a menu of services. The DHCS Medi-Cal Dental provider listing shows how much this varies city by city: the most frequently listed non-English language among providers was Spanish for Los Angeles, Long Beach and Pasadena billing addresses, but Armenian for Glendale.

## California privacy law and your practice website

The **California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)**, as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act, is a consideration for practice websites in a way that has no direct equivalent in Texas, Florida or Georgia. According to the California Attorney General, it applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California and meet at least one threshold: annual gross revenue above a set amount, buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more California residents or households, or deriving half or more of annual revenue from selling personal information. The California Privacy Protection Agency lists the revenue threshold as $26.625 million effective 1 January 2025. The law exempts certain medical information.

<SourceNote ids={["ca-oag-ccpa", "cppa-ccpa-faq"]} />

We do not make compliance judgements. A single-office practice may be below every threshold; a larger group or DSO may not be. Even where most patient data is exempt medical information, the marketing layer is where questions tend to arise: advertising pixels, analytics tags and session-recording tools can send visitor data to third parties. The Attorney General's page also says covered businesses must honour a user-enabled global privacy control, such as Global Privacy Control, as an opt-out request. Your counsel should decide what applies to you.

## Call recording and call tracking

Many new patients still phone, so call tracking matters, and many call-tracking platforms also offer recording. The Legislative Counsel's digest of a 2015 bill summarised the law then in force as making it a crime to record a confidential communication, including a telephone call, without the consent of all parties.

<SourceNote ids={["ca-ab925-2015-digest-pc632"]} />

That digest is a 2015 summary, not the current text of Penal Code 632, so treat it as a reason to check with counsel, not as legal guidance. Our approach is to measure calls without recording by default: tracking numbers, call duration, missed-call rates and a simple outcome tag entered by your team. Recording is enabled only after your counsel approves the disclosure, and recordings are never used in reports that include patient details. The [dental call tracking guide](/blog/dental-call-tracking/) explains how to count calls without treating every one as a new patient.

## Where to start

If your practice is in Los Angeles County, the most useful first step is a diagnosis of your own data rather than a county-wide plan. The [Dental Growth Audit](/dental-marketing-audit/) reviews how patients find, judge and contact your practice: your Business Profile, website and booking path, any ad spend, and whether your measurement can tell an inquiry from an attended visit. For groups with offices in several cities, it also checks whether each location has its own accurate profile and page. The [dental marketing metrics guide](/resources/dental-marketing-metrics/) defines the measures we use, so you can compare our findings with any other proposal you receive.
