Negatives are the counterpart to match types. How positive keywords match searches is covered in [choosing match types for a dental account](/blog/dental-google-ads-keyword-match-types/); this article is about the exclusions.

## How negative keywords actually match

Google states plainly that negative match types "work differently than their positive counterparts". The main difference is that you need to add synonyms and singular or plural versions yourself if you want to exclude them, because negatives do not match close variants or other expansions. Google's example: a negative broad match keyword "flowers" stops ads on "red flowers" but not on "red flower". Google also says negatives automatically account for casing and misspellings, so those do not need adding separately.

<SourceNote ids={["google-ads-negative-keywords"]} />

The three negative match types follow these rules:

- **Negative broad** (the default): the ad will not show if the search contains all of the negative's terms, in any order. It may still show if only some of the terms are present.
- **Negative phrase**: the ad will not show if the search contains all the terms in the same order, even with extra words around them.
- **Negative exact**: the ad will not show only if the search is exactly those terms, in that order, with no extra words.

Google also notes that a negative keyword appearing after the 16th word of a very long search may not block the ad.

The table shows why negatives cannot be set once and forgotten. A practice that excludes "dental jobs" still pays for "dental job openings" and "dental assistant careers" unless it also excludes job, careers and their variants. ## A three-layer framework for dental negatives

Google provides three places for negatives, each with a different reach. Using them deliberately is the difference between a clean account and one where nobody knows why an ad stopped showing.

**Account level.** Google states that an account-level negative list applies automatically to search and shopping inventory in Search, Performance Max, App, Shopping, Smart and Local campaigns, with a limit of 1,000 negatives. Because of that reach, it should only hold searches the practice would never want in any campaign. A word that is wrong for one campaign and right for another does not belong here.

**Shared lists.** Google allows negative keyword lists of up to 5,000 negatives each and up to 20 lists per account, applied to selected campaigns, so one edit updates every campaign sharing the list. Organise them by theme so that someone can tell later why a word was excluded.

**Campaign and ad group.** These are the most precise tools, and the only ones suited to routing. If an account has an implant ad group and a denture ad group, adding denture terms as negatives in the implant ad group pushes those searches toward the ad group and landing page built for them. That is a routing decision, not an exclusion; the searches are still wanted.

The pattern in the table is that single-word broad negatives carry the most risk. "Free" as a broad negative blocks every search containing it. ## Finding negatives from your own search terms

The search terms report is the source of a practice's negatives. Google describes it as showing the searches that triggered ads, with a Keyword column (added via the columns menu) showing which keyword matched. It lists only terms used by a significant number of people, and omits low-volume terms for privacy, so it never shows everything.

A short weekly review of the report is usually more effective than a long list added at launch. Each review should record what was added, at which layer and why, so the list can be audited later.

## Tradeoffs and common mistakes

Fewer negatives means more spend on searches the practice cannot use. More negatives, especially broad single words, means fewer impressions and the risk of silently excluding valuable searches, which never appear in any report because the ad never showed. The second error is harder to see than the first, which is why it persists.

Common mistakes:

- Importing a generic negative list without reading it against the practice's services and insurance.
- Putting routing terms at account level, which also blocks them in the campaign built to serve them.
- Assuming a negative covers plurals and synonyms.
- Using a negative to fix a landing page or schedule problem.
- Never reviewing old negatives after the practice adds a service, extends hours or joins a plan.
