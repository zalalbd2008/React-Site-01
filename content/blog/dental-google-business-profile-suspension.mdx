Google allows only one appeal per decision, so submit everything at once.</KeyAnswer>

A suspended profile disappears from Google Search and Maps at exactly the moment patients are looking for the practice. The temptation is to appeal immediately, or to create a new listing. Both usually make things worse. This article explains what to check first, which documents Google says can strengthen an appeal, the address and signage rules that most often trip up dental offices, and what to do after a decision. It covers evidence and preparation; the wider question of when a dentist should have their own profile is in [practice versus practitioner profiles](/blog/dentist-practice-google-business-profiles/).

## Three things not to do first

1. **Do not create a new profile.** Google's suspension and appeal pages both say not to create a new Business Profile for the same business. A replacement is likely to be treated as a duplicate.
2. **Do not appeal before fixing the cause.** Google's suspension page begins its guidance by asking you to make sure the profile follows all the guidelines, and notes that appeals can only be submitted once.
3. **Do not make unrelated edits during the process.** Business Profile Help warns that changing a category may require re-verification, and that editing the name, address or category while a verification postcard is pending invalidates the code. Change only what the violation requires.

## Identify exactly which decision you are appealing

Different restrictions have different routes. Open the Google Business Profile appeals tool while signed in to the account that manages the profile. Google says the tool shows the date of the decision, the reason, a link to the violated policy, whether it can be appealed and its status. Write these down before doing anything else.

The account point matters for practices that use agencies or have had several managers over the years. Google's suspension page says that if a manager's Google Account is removed, that manager is suspended on the profiles they manage, but the locations are not affected; if the owner's account is removed, all locations owned by that user are removed. Knowing which account holds ownership tells you who must file the appeal.

## Fix the cause before you appeal

Google does not always say in detail what triggered a suspension, but the policy link in the appeals tool usually points to a section of the guidelines. For dental practices, the sections below are the ones most often relevant.

### Business name

Google says the name should reflect the real-world name used consistently on the storefront, website, stationery and as known to customers. Its examples of what a name must not include cover marketing taglines, phone numbers or URLs, and service or product information. A profile named with a treatment and a city, when the sign on the door shows only the practice name, is a likely problem. Special characters or legal terms such as "PLLC" need real-world proof, such as signage, business cards or invoices, that shows them as part of the name.

### Address, suite and signage

The address rules are where dental offices in medical buildings, shared suites and new developments most often run into trouble. Google's guidelines say:

- Use a precise, accurate address. P.O. boxes or mailboxes at remote locations are not acceptable.
- Put a suite or unit number in address line 2.
- A virtual office (a rented mailing address the business does not operate from) is not eligible.
- An office in a co-working space needs clear signage, must receive customers there during business hours and must be staffed during those hours by the business's own staff.
- Businesses showing their address should maintain permanent fixed signage of the business name at the address.
- There should be one profile per location; departments and individual practitioners may have separate pages under specific rules.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-guidelines", "google-gbp-address"]} />

### Practitioner and practice profiles

Google's guidelines name dentists as individual practitioners and set conditions for when a dentist may have a separate profile and how it must be named. If the suspended profile is a practitioner profile, or sits at the same address as one, check it against those rules before appealing: a departed associate's profile that still carries the practice name is a typical problem. The eligibility and naming rules are set out in full in [practice versus practitioner profiles](/blog/dentist-practice-google-business-profiles/).

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-guidelines"]} />

### Duplicates and relocations

If the practice moved, Google says to update the existing profile with the new address, not create a new one. If you created a duplicate by mistake, removing it deletes its content and linked managers, so be certain which profile you remove.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-duplicates"]} />

### Reviews

If the restriction relates to reviews, Google's prohibited content policy forbids offering incentives for reviews, discouraging negative reviews and selectively soliciting positive ones. Stopping the practice before appealing, and being able to describe how reviews are now requested, is part of the preparation. The process is covered in [requesting reviews without incentives or gating](/blog/dental-review-request-process/).

## The evidence pack

Google's suspension and appeal pages list evidence that can strengthen an appeal and give one firm instruction: the business name and address on each document should match the profile. Prepare the pack before opening the tool; Google's appeal page recommends preparing documents before submitting.

Two notes on what is and is not on Google's list.

**Professional licences.** Google's list names a business licence, not a professional licence. A state dental licence identifies a dentist rather than a business location. It may be relevant to a practitioner profile, but Google does not say so, and it should supplement, not replace, the documents Google names.

**Signage photos.** Signage photos are not on the suspension-evidence list. Google does ask for evidence of permanent signage when appealing a wrongly merged duplicate, and its live video verification guidance asks businesses to be ready to show outside signs, equipment and staff-only areas. Having clear photos ready costs nothing and helps if support asks for them.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-duplicates", "google-gbp-verify"]} />

**Keep patient information out.** Choose documents that show business details only. Redact anything on a bill or letter that identifies a patient, and never upload schedules, charts or images that include patients.

## Submitting the appeal

Google's process is short, which is why preparation matters:

1. Open the appeals tool, confirm the account and select the profile.
2. Select the decision you want to appeal and review the reason and policy link.
3. Upload supporting files with "Choose file"; multiple files can be attached.
4. Use the text box to explain why the profile should be reinstated.
5. Tick the confirmation that all supporting evidence is attached and submit.

Google says it will email the decision, that appeal reviews and decisions can take up to 5 business days, and that you should not submit multiple appeals for the same issue before a decision.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-suspended-profiles", "google-gbp-appeals"]} />

Keep the explanation factual and brief: the practice's real name, that it treats patients at the address during the stated hours, what was out of line with the guidelines, what you changed and which documents are attached. Avoid arguing about competitors or the fairness of the decision.

## After the decision

The appeals tool shows a status of submitted, approved, not approved, can't be appealed or eligible for appeal. If the request is denied, Google says it may be able to do an additional review, and you can provide evidence that was not included with the original appeal. That is a second chance, not a routine step, so the first submission should be complete. If the profile is reinstated and something is still wrong, Google directs businesses to Business Profile support.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-suspended-profiles"]} />

### For dental groups with many suspended offices

When more than 10 profiles are affected, Google's process asks whether the request is for bulk reinstatement and asks for a spreadsheet listing each suspended location's Business Profile ID with supporting documentation such as registration, licence, tax certificates or utility bills. Google notes that the status in the tool only updates for the profile you selected. Groups that have acquired practices should check that each office's documents are in the name shown on its profile, which is often not the case straight after an acquisition. ## A worked example: suspended after moving suites

<Illustrative title="A practice suspended shortly after moving within the same building">
A general practice moves from suite 110 to suite 240 in the same medical office building and updates its profile address. Two weeks later the profile is suspended. The appeals tool links to the guidelines on representing the business accurately.

**Diagnosis.** The new address on the profile has the suite number in address line 1. The building directory still lists suite 110, and the new suite has only a temporary paper sign. The utility bills are still addressed to the practice owner's management company, not the practice name.

**Fixes before appealing.** Move the suite number to address line 2. Install permanent suite signage and update the building directory. Request the utility account be put in the practice name.

**Evidence pack.** The state business registration in the practice name; the new lease page showing suite 240, with financial terms redacted; the first utility bill in the practice name at suite 240; photos of the permanent suite sign and directory, held in reserve.

**Explanation text.** Three sentences: the practice moved suites in the same building on a stated date, it treats patients at suite 240 during the hours shown, and the attached documents show the name and new address.

The details are invented to show how cause, fix and evidence line up.
</Illustrative>

## Tradeoffs and common mistakes

- **Speed versus completeness.** Appealing on day one with partial evidence uses the one appeal Google allows. Waiting a few days to obtain a matching document is usually the better trade.
- **Mismatched names.** A registration in the owner's personal name or a holding company, with no link to the practice's trading name, does not match the profile. Gather documents that show the trading name, or explain the relationship.
- **Agency-owned profiles.** If only an agency account owns the profile, the practice depends on the agency to appeal. Google's third-party policies say businesses must retain ownership or co-ownership at all times.
- **Fixing only the profile.** If the website, building directory and signage still show the old name or suite, the inconsistency remains.
- **Repeated appeals.** Google asks businesses not to submit multiple appeals for the same issue before a decision.
