<KeyAnswer>To keep visibility through an acquisition, rebrand or migration, map every old URL to its closest new equivalent, serve permanent server-side redirects, use Search Console's Change of Address tool for domain moves, and change each office's Business Profile name only once real-world signage matches. Sequence the changes rather than launching them together, and keep redirects for at least a year.</KeyAnswer>

This guide covers the mechanics of change: moving, renaming and merging. Choosing the long-term structure for a group's sites is a separate decision, covered in [subfolders, subdomains or separate domains](/blog/multi-location-dental-website-structure/).

## Which kind of change are you making?

"Rebrand" covers several different projects, and each puts different assets at risk. Identify which you are doing before planning anything.

| Change | What moves | Main risks | Google tools involved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acquisition, brand kept | Accounts and ownership, not URLs | Losing registrar, hosting or profile access | Business Profile ownership transfer |
| Rename on the same domain | Name, logo, copy; sometimes URLs | Profile name mismatch; site name in search lagging | Business Profile edit; WebSite structured data |
| Domain change | Every URL | Broken links, lost rankings during recrawl | Redirects; Change of Address tool |
| Acquired site folded into group domain | One domain into a path on another | Unmapped pages, duplicated content | Redirects; Change of Address tool to a path |
| CMS or design rebuild | Templates, often URLs | Accidental URL changes, removed pages, noindex left on | Redirects if URLs change; sitemaps |
| Office relocation | Address | Profile re-verification, map pin, citations | Business Profile edit and re-verification |

A group integrating an acquisition may be doing three of these at once. The point of the table is to separate them so they can be scheduled one after another.

## How Google describes a site move

Google's guide to site moves with URL changes sets out a sequence: prepare and test the new site, prepare a mapping from current URLs to new ones, configure the server to redirect old URLs to new ones, then monitor traffic on both. It recommends splitting large moves into smaller steps, changing only one thing at a time — for example moving to a new domain first, then changing layout — and timing the move for a quieter period.

Google also sets expectations. It says to expect temporary ranking fluctuation while it recrawls and reindexes, and that for medium-sized sites it can take a few weeks or more for new URLs to replace old ones, longer for larger sites. It states that 301 and other permanent redirects do not cause a loss in PageRank, and that a move is complete only once Googlebot has visited every old and new URL at least once.

<SourceNote ids={["google-site-move-url-changes"]} />

On redirect types, Google's redirects documentation distinguishes permanent redirects (HTTP 301 or 308), which show the new target in search results, from temporary ones, which keep showing the source. A rebrand or domain move is permanent, so the redirects should be too. Server-side redirects are preferred; JavaScript redirects are a fallback only when server-side or meta refresh options are unavailable.

## When to use the Change of Address tool — and when not to

The Search Console Change of Address tool tells Google you have moved from one domain or subdomain to another, and Google says it helps migrate search results to the new site. The help page sets out conditions that matter for dental groups:

- **Use it after** the move is live and redirects are working, not before.
- **It works only at domain level.** You can move example.com or m.example.com, not a path such as example.com/office/. You can, however, move a whole domain to a path on another domain — the usual case when an acquired practice's site is folded into a group site.
- **It does not move subdomains below the one you specify**, including www, so each variant you have used must be verified and submitted.
- **Do not chain moves.** After moving site A to site B, you cannot immediately submit another move from B to C. Settle the destination before you start.
- **The period is 180 days.** Google says it forwards signals and prefers the new site for 180 days; after that it no longer recognises a relationship between the sites. Keep redirects at least that long, and keep paying for the old domain for at least a year so it cannot be bought and misused.

For moves within one domain — say from /our-offices/ to /locations/ — the help says to add redirects and update sitemaps; the tool is not needed.

## Business Profiles during a rename or acquisition

The website is only half of local visibility. Each office's Google Business Profile carries its name, reviews and map position, and Google's rules constrain how and when it can change.

**Name timing.** Google's guidelines say a business name should reflect its real-world name as used consistently on the storefront, website and stationery. The edit-profile help adds that if you change the business name after verification, you might need to verify again, and that changes are reviewed before going live. In practice that means the profile name changes when the signage does — not weeks before as a teaser, and not months after.

**During verification.** The verification help warns not to edit the business name, address or category while waiting for a postcard verification code, because the code sent will not work. Avoid stacking a name change on top of a pending verification.

**Chains.** For groups converting several offices to one brand, the guidelines expect all locations of a chain to use the same name unless the real-world name consistently varies. Rolling a new name across offices office by office is fine, as long as each profile matches that office's signage at the time.

**Ownership after an acquisition.** Business Profiles have owners and managers, with a single primary owner. Primary ownership can be transferred to another owner or manager already on the profile; the help notes that a new owner or manager has to wait 7 days before some actions, including transferring primary ownership. Get the group added as owner and then made primary before any other change.

**Relocation.** The edit help states that if a business moves to a new address after verification, it must be verified again.

## Building the URL map

The URL map is the single most important document in a migration. Every URL on the old site that has traffic, links or a purpose gets a row and a decision.

Google's site move guide also asks you to update internal links to point at new URLs directly, check rel="canonical" annotations on the new site, and make sure the new robots.txt and noindex settings reflect what should be indexed. Its canonicalisation documentation lists redirects and rel="canonical" as strong signals, so the two should agree.

## Launch-day and first-month checks

<Checklist title="Rebrand launch checks" items={[

  "The new site has no leftover noindex tags or staging robots.txt blocks",
  "Every new location page shows the same name, address, phone and hours as its Business Profile",
  "Business Profile website links point to the office's location page on the new domain",
  "Call tracking numbers and booking links on the new pages route to the right office",
  "Conversion events still fire after the template change",
  "Search Console shows the new sitemap read and the Change of Address in progress where used",
  "WebSite structured data on the new home page carries the new brand name"

The last item relates to site names. Google says site names are chosen from signals including WebSite structured data, the home page title and other text, that it cannot manually change an automatically selected site name, and that it supports an alternateName property. Adding the new name consistently, and the old one as an alternate during transition, is a reasonable way to indicate a preference; how quickly the displayed name changes is not something a practice controls.

## Common mistakes in dental migrations

- **Redirecting everything to the home page.** Patients looking for a provider bio or forms land on a generic page, and Google gets no page-level mapping.
- **Renaming profiles before signage changes.** A name that does not match the storefront conflicts with Google's naming guidelines and confuses patients arriving at the old sign.
- **Letting the old domain expire.** Google's help specifically recommends keeping it for at least a year to prevent misuse by others.
- **Bundling domain, design, CMS and URL changes.** When traffic falls, nobody can tell which change caused it.
- **Losing measurement.** New templates often drop tracking scripts or change form behaviour. Compare calls and bookings per office before and after, as described in [the group dashboard guide](/blog/dental-group-marketing-dashboard/).
- **Forgetting third-party directories.** Insurer directories and dental association listings often carry the old name long after the website changes.

## Sources and limitations

The Google periods quoted — at least one year for redirects in the site move guide, 180 days for Change of Address — were current on 8 November 2026. Google describes ranking fluctuation during moves but does not promise a recovery time, and none can be promised here. Business Profile reviews of name changes and re-verification outcomes vary by profile. Transferring domains and profiles in an acquisition also has contractual aspects your counsel should handle.
