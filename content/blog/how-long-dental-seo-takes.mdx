<KeyAnswer>There is no fixed timeline. Google's SEO Starter Guide says some changes take effect in a few hours and others could take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before assessing a change. For a practice, the useful question is what should move first: indexing, then search impressions and Business Profile actions, then inquiries, then booked and attended visits.</KeyAnswer>

<SourceNote ids={["google-seo-starter-guide"]} />

Practice owners are often told SEO "takes six months" or "takes a year". Neither figure is a measurement; both are rules of thumb, and they say nothing about your practice. This article sets out what Google itself states, why timelines differ so much between practices, and an order of indicators to watch so that you can judge progress long before a final verdict on new patients is possible.

## What Google actually says about timing

Google makes three statements that bear on timing, and each is narrower than the way it is often repeated.

The **SEO Starter Guide**, last updated 10 December 2025, says: "Every change you make will take some time to be reflected on Google's end. Some changes might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months. In general, you likely want to wait a few weeks to assess whether your work had beneficial effects in Google Search results." It adds that not every change will have a noticeable impact.

The **"Do you need an SEO?"** page on Search Central is often cited as giving a range of months. The version we retrieved on 8 November 2026, last updated 5 June 2026, does not contain a month range. It suggests asking a prospective SEO "What kind of results do you expect to see, and in what timeframe? How do you measure your success?" and says an audit should give "realistic estimates of improvement". In other words, Google puts the onus on the provider to state a timeframe and a measure, not on a universal figure.

Google's guide to **how Search works** adds a qualifier that matters for expectations: Google does not promise to crawl, index or serve any page, even if it follows Google's guidance.

<SourceNote ids={["google-seo-starter-guide", "google-do-you-need-seo", "google-how-search-works"]} />

So the honest answer to "how long?" is: it depends on what needs to change, and you should agree in advance what will be measured and when.

## Why timelines differ between practices

The same plan can show results in weeks for one practice and take much longer for another. The starting point explains most of the difference.

Business Profile timings are worth knowing because they are among the few that Google states. Business Profile Help says profile changes usually show in about 10 minutes but can take up to 30 days, and that most postcard verification codes arrive within 14 days. Editing the name, address or category while waiting for a postcard code invalidates the code. A practice that needs re-verification, or whose profile is suspended, cannot expect local improvements until that is resolved; see [what to prepare before appealing a suspension](/blog/dental-google-business-profile-suspension/).

## What to measure first, in order

Because the final outcomes (booked and attended new-patient visits) arrive last and depend on things outside SEO, the work has to be judged in stages. Each stage is a precondition for the next.

### Stage 1: Indexing

The first check is whether the pages you care about are in Google's index, under the URL you intend. The Page indexing report in Search Console shows which URLs are indexed and why others are not; URL Inspection shows the status of a single page and which URL Google chose as canonical. After technical fixes, this is the earliest place to see change.

<SourceNote ids={["gsc-page-indexing-report", "gsc-url-inspection"]} />

### Stage 2: Impressions for the searches you target

Impressions are counted when a page appears in results, whether or not anyone clicks. The Search Console Performance report shows impressions, clicks and average position by query and page. For a rewritten implant page, the question is whether it starts appearing for implant-related searches, even at a low position. Rising impressions are an early sign that Google considers the page a match.

<SourceNote ids={["gsc-performance-report"]} />

### Stage 3: Clicks and Business Profile actions

For local searches, the profile's own Performance section reports views, the search terms people used, direction requests, calls and website clicks. Google's definitions matter: "calls" counts taps on the call button in the profile, not answered or completed calls; the searches metric is updated at the start of each month and can take up to 5 days to appear; and the data includes views and actions from both organic results and Google Ads.

<SourceNote ids={["google-gbp-performance"]} />

### Stages 4 to 6: Inquiries, qualified inquiries and visits

Only here does marketing meet the practice's own records. Count calls, forms and booking requests separately, then follow them through to booked and attended visits. This requires call tracking, form tracking and some matching with the practice management system, usually as monthly totals rather than patient-level data. Definitions for each stage are in the [dental marketing metrics guide](/resources/dental-marketing-metrics/), and the set-up is described on our [conversion tracking service page](/services/dental-conversion-tracking/).

## A worked example of reading the early months

<Illustrative title="Reviewing month four for a practice targeting implants and emergency visits">
A single-office practice began SEO four months ago. The owner asks whether it is working.

**Indexing.** Two treatment pages that were blocked by a noindex tag in month one are now indexed under the intended URLs. That is stage 1 achieved.

**Impressions.** Search Console shows the implant page moved from almost no impressions to around 600 a month for implant-related searches, at an average position of 18. The emergency page shows impressions, but almost all for searches outside the practice's area. Stage 2 is achieved for implants and only partly for emergencies.

**Profile actions.** Direction requests and calls from the profile are slightly up on the same months last year, but the practice also started a small Google Ads campaign, and profile data includes both organic and ad activity. No conclusion can yet be drawn about SEO's share.

**Inquiries.** The practice counts new-patient calls on a paper tally. There is no split by source. Stage 4 cannot be measured.

**Decision.** Keep the implant work; rewrite the emergency page to state hours and location more clearly; start tracking calls by source before month six, so that the next review can judge inquiries rather than guess.

All figures are invented to show how the stages are read.
</Illustrative>

## When slow progress is a warning sign

Patience is reasonable; open-ended patience is not. The questions below separate "not yet" from "not working".

<Checklist title="Signs an SEO plan needs reviewing, not more time" items={[
  "Pages that were meant to be fixed are still not indexed after the work was reported done",
  "No rise in impressions for target searches after priority pages were published and indexed",
  "Reports show only rankings for keywords the provider chose, with no impressions or inquiry data",
  "The Business Profile is still unverified, suspended or duplicated months into the work",
  "Nobody can say how many inquiries came from organic search or the profile",
  "The plan has not changed despite evidence that one part is not working"
]} />

If an otherwise healthy site suddenly loses traffic, Google's guide to debugging traffic drops lists the main causes to check: algorithmic updates, technical issues, security issues, spam or manual actions, and seasonality or changing interest. Seasonality is easy to forget in dentistry, where demand can shift with school terms, holidays and benefit-year timing; compare with the same period last year before drawing conclusions.

## Common mistakes in judging SEO timelines

- **Judging by one personal search.** Location and personalisation make single searches unreliable; use Search Console and profile data over weeks.
- **Changing everything at once.** Rewriting pages, moving the site and editing the profile in the same month makes it impossible to tell what worked.
- **Counting every call as a new patient.** Existing patients, suppliers and wrong numbers all call. Separate new-patient inquiries before comparing months.
- **Comparing month to month only.** Year-on-year comparison removes some seasonal effects.
- **Waiting for a provider's timeline without agreeing measures.** Google suggests asking what results to expect and how success is measured; agree both at the start.

## Sources and limitations

Timing statements in this article are Google's, quoted from pages retrieved on 8 November 2026; Google may revise them. We found no Google statement of a typical number of months for SEO, and we do not offer one. Business Profile metrics are defined by Google and can change; "calls" counts button taps, not completed calls. The timeline and worked example are illustrative and do not describe results achieved for any practice. Practice-side factors, especially call handling and appointment capacity, affect stages 4 to 6 regardless of SEO.
