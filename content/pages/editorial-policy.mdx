## Who this content is for

Everything on this site is written for the people who make marketing decisions for dental practices: owners, dentists evaluating an agency, practice managers, and the marketing and operations leads at dental groups and DSOs. It is not written for patients, and it is not dental or medical advice.

Our aim is for each page to help a reader make a real business decision — what to fix first, how to compare proposals, what to measure — and to be clear about the evidence behind each statement.

## Research and sourcing standards

We research before we write. Each page starts with a research brief that records the question the page answers, the evidence available, and the gaps we could not fill.

- **Primary sources first.** For platform behavior we cite the platform's own current documentation, such as Google Search Central, Google Ads Help and Google Business Profile Help. For regulation we cite the authority, such as the FTC or HHS. For demographics we cite government data, such as the Census Bureau.
- **Read before citing.** We cite a page only after opening it and checking that it says what we attribute to it. We do not cite from memory or from someone else's summary.
- **Record what a source measures.** Every source is kept in a register with its publisher, URL, publication date where given, retrieval date, geography and limitations. Cited sources are listed at the end of each page.
- **State the retrieval date.** Platform guidance changes. Where a page describes how a platform works, it reflects the documentation as retrieved on the date recorded, and we say so where change is likely.

## How we handle statistics

A number without its denominator, geography and period can mislead, so we keep all three with it.

- Every statistic names its source, and we keep the original scope. A national or state figure is never presented as a city figure.
- We do not publish search volumes, cost-per-click figures, market sizes, conversion benchmarks or traffic forecasts unless they come from a cited source with a date and scope.
- Keywords we discuss are research candidates, not claims about demand, unless a dated tool measurement is cited.
- We do not infer things the data does not show — for example, cosmetic demand from household income, or language preference from demographics. Where such a link might matter, we present it as a hypothesis for a practice to test with its own data.

## Illustrative examples are always labelled

Many pages include worked examples with invented numbers, because they make a calculation or trade-off easier to follow. These always appear inside a box marked as illustrative. An illustrative example is not a client result, a benchmark or a forecast, and its figures should not be quoted as data.

## No invented experience or results

We do not claim experience or results we cannot evidence. That means no unattributed claims about what clients typically see, no testimonials or ratings without written permission, and no case studies without a stated baseline, measurement period, data source and the other factors that could explain the outcome. Observations of other websites are described in aggregate, with the date and sample size, and never name or criticise a specific practice or agency.

## Clinical scope

Our content covers marketing operations: visibility, advertising, websites, reputation and measurement. It does not give clinical or treatment advice. When we write content for a client practice that includes clinical statements, that content is reviewed and approved by the practice's own clinicians before publication, and responsibility for its clinical accuracy rests with them.

## How AI assistance is used

Content on this site is drafted with the assistance of AI tools and then reviewed against the sources cited on the page. AI assistance is used to speed up drafting and structuring; it does not replace checking. Factual claims are checked against their cited sources before a page is marked as fact-checked, and a page moves through several status stages — drafted, fact-checked, then reviewed for usefulness and accuracy — before it is published.

AI tools can produce plausible statements that are wrong. That is why we do not publish statistics, platform claims or company facts that have not been traced to a source or to our own verified business records.

## Bylines and review

Pages are published under the Alpha Dental Media editorial team byline. We will add named authors and reviewers only when their identity, role and credentials have been verified; until then we do not attach a reviewer to any page, because claiming a review that did not happen would be misleading. Named leadership will be listed on the [leadership page](/leadership/) once biographies are approved. More about the company is on the [about page](/about/).

## Dates

Each page shows when it was first published. We change the updated date only when a page has a substantive change, such as new or corrected information, revised guidance or a materially different recommendation. Fixing a typo, adjusting layout or rewording without changing meaning does not count as an update, and we do not refresh dates to make content look newer than it is.

## Corrections

If you believe something on this site is wrong, out of date or misattributed, please tell us through the [contact form](/contact/). Include the page address, the statement in question and, if possible, a source that shows the correct information.

We check each request against the evidence. Where a correction is warranted, we fix the page and add a note on it describing what changed and the date of the change. Where we disagree, we keep the page as it is but will consider adding context if the point is reasonable. Corrections that change a page's substance also update its updated date.

## Conflicts of interest and advertising

We do not accept payment for placements, links or mentions in our content, and we do not publish sponsored articles. When we mention a tool or platform, it is because it is relevant to the reader's decision, not because of a commercial arrangement. Where our own services are relevant to a topic, pages link to them openly, and the commercial interest is obvious: we are a dental marketing agency, and we would like you to consider working with us.

We also apply these standards to comparisons. Tools such as the [agency comparison scorecard](/resources/dental-agency-comparison-scorecard/) are designed to be usable for evaluating any provider, including us. How we gather and record observational research is explained in the [research methodology](/resources/research-methodology/).
