## The five stages at a glance

Every engagement, whatever the service, moves through the same five stages. The stages repeat: a review feeds the next round of diagnosis, so the program improves on evidence rather than on a fixed annual plan.

## Stage 1: Diagnose

**The diagnosis establishes where your practice stands today and where potential patients are being lost.** We look at visibility, the website, advertising and measurement together, because a symptom in one often has its cause in another. The output is a baseline and a short list of the problems that matter most.

We start by agreeing what you want: which services you want more of, which patients you cannot currently accept, and roughly how many new patients the schedule can take. Then we look at how the practice appears in search and maps for those services, how the website handles a visitor on a phone, what any ad account is paying for, and how inquiries are tracked.

The most important part is the baseline. We record what can be measured now — search visibility, website visits, calls, forms, bookings — and, where your practice management system allows, how many new patients actually attended. Without a baseline, no later result can be judged.

### What we need from you at this stage

- Your goals, priority services, and any services or patient types you want fewer of
- An honest estimate of new-patient capacity and where the schedule is tight
- Every location's address, and any planned openings, closures, acquisitions or rebrands
- View-level access to Search Console, Analytics and any ad accounts, and manager access to each Business Profile
- Summary new-patient numbers from your own systems — totals only, never patient names or records

Each platform has its own permission levels, so the practice can grant only what a stage needs. Google Ads offers Read-only, Standard and Admin access, among others; a Business Profile has owners, who can add or remove users, and managers, who cannot; Search Console distinguishes owners from full and restricted users. For diagnosis, Read-only access in Google Ads and restricted-user access in Search Console are enough. A Business Profile has no view-only role, so the smallest grant there is manager access, which can edit the profile; diagnosis does not require any edits to be made. Owner-level control should stay with the practice.

### Decision point

At the end of the diagnosis you decide whether to go further. Sometimes the right answer is a narrow fix your own team can make, or no marketing change at all because the constraint is capacity or phone handling.

## Stage 2: Plan

**The plan turns findings into a sequenced list of changes, each with a reason, a dependency and a way to measure it.** It also says what we would leave alone. You approve the plan before any work starts.

Priorities are set by three tests: how much a fix is likely to affect qualified inquiries or bookings, what it costs in money and practice time, and what it depends on. Measurement fixes usually come first, because they make every later change judgeable. Changes that need the practice — new photos, clinician review of treatment pages, a change to phone coverage — are scheduled around your availability rather than assumed.

The plan defines success at each stage of the funnel: visits, inquiries, qualified inquiries, bookings and attended visits. Where a measure cannot yet be tracked, the plan says so and states what would be needed to track it.

### What we need from you at this stage

- One named decision-maker who can approve the plan and later changes
- Confirmation of budget limits, including any ad spend
- Agreement on who in the practice will answer questions about calls and bookings

### Decision point

You choose which items to fund and in what order. A plan can be phased: it is often sensible to fix measurement and conversion before spending more on visibility.

## Stage 3: Implement

**Implementation carries out the approved changes in your accounts and on your website, in the agreed order, with every change logged.** Anything public-facing that makes claims about your practice — treatment descriptions, prices, insurance, hours — is approved by you before it goes live.

Typical work includes correcting tracking, updating Business Profile details and categories, restructuring ad campaigns and their geographic and schedule settings, improving booking and call paths, and writing or revising pages. Clinical statements in any content are reviewed by your own clinicians; our role is marketing, not clinical advice.

The change log matters as much as the change. It records what was done and when, so a later shift in results can be lined up against what actually changed — ours or the practice's.

### What we need from you at this stage

- Edit access where changes are agreed, granted through your own accounts
- Timely approvals and clinician review of content
- Notice of anything that changes in the practice: new dentists, new hours, a moved phone line, a closed office

### Decision point

If something found during implementation changes the case for a planned item — a profile issue, a technical constraint, a capacity change — we raise it and you decide whether to adjust the plan.

## Stage 4: Measure

**Measurement compares results against the baseline and separates each stage of the funnel, so you can see where change is and is not happening.** It also records the things outside marketing that could explain a change, such as a new hire, a holiday period or a closed competitor.

We report inquiries, qualified inquiries, bookings and attended visits as separate figures, because a rise in one does not imply a rise in the next. Where qualification or attendance needs your front desk's input, we agree a simple way to capture it. Where Google Ads is involved, outcomes recorded after the click, such as a booked appointment, can be imported back into the account as offline conversions, so the campaign is judged on more than form fills.

### What a report contains

- **What was done**, from the change log, in plain language
- **What changed**, against the baseline, at each funnel stage
- **Spend and cost per stage**, where advertising is involved, including cost per attended visit when attendance can be measured
- **What is known, estimated and unknown**, stated separately
- **Outside factors** that may have affected the numbers
- **Recommendations**, with the evidence for each

## Stage 5: Review

**The review is a conversation about the evidence, ending in a decision: continue, change or stop.** We walk through the report and the change log together, check the conclusions against what your team saw in the practice, and update the plan.

Some reviews end with more investment in what worked. Others end by stopping an activity that is not producing qualified inquiries, or by shifting effort from visibility to conversion because the bottleneck moved. The updated plan becomes the starting point for the next cycle, and the baseline is refreshed so comparisons stay fair.

### What we need from you at this stage

- Time with the decision-maker and, ideally, whoever manages the front desk
- Your view of what changed in the practice during the period
- Any feedback from patients or staff about how they found or contacted the practice
