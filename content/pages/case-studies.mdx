There are no case studies on this page yet. That is deliberate. We will publish a result only when a client has agreed to it in writing and the evidence meets the standard below. Until then, the honest answer to "what results do you get?" is that we can show you our method, not someone else's numbers.

## Why we hold case studies to a written standard

A marketing result is easy to state and hard to interpret. "Leads doubled" can mean twice as many new patients, or the same patients counted by a new tracking system. A single strong example can also suggest that every practice should expect the same. The FTC's endorsement guides note that an advertised customer experience is likely to be read as representative of what customers generally achieve, and that, without substantiation, the advertiser should clearly disclose what people can generally expect. We apply that thinking to every result we publish.

## What every case study must contain

<Checklist title="Required before any case study is published" items={[
  "Written client permission, recorded with the date and the exact content approved, including whether the practice may be named",
  "Context: practice type, number of locations, market and the problem the practice wanted solved",
  "A baseline period with dates and the same measures used afterwards",
  "A measurement period with dates, long enough to cover normal variation",
  "The intervention: what was changed, when, and by whom, including work done by the practice itself",
  "Measurement definitions for each figure, such as what counted as a qualified lead or an attended visit",
  "The data source for each figure: ad platform, analytics, call tracking or the practice management system",
  "Results reported separately at each stage rather than combined into one headline",
  "Attribution limits: what the data can and cannot show about cause",
  "Confounders: anything else that changed in the same period",
  "Patient privacy: totals only, with no patient names, images or treatment details"
]} />

### Results reported stage by stage

Each case study keeps these measures apart, because they can move in different directions:

| Stage | What it shows | Typical data source |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic and visibility | How many people saw or visited | Search Console, Business Profile, analytics |
| Calls and forms | How many contacted the practice | Call tracking, form logs |
| Qualified leads | How many were prospective new patients for offered services | Practice review of calls and forms |
| Booked appointments | How many scheduled a first visit | Practice management system |
| Attended visits | How many were seen | Practice management system |
| Treatment acceptance | How many accepted recommended treatment, where relevant | Practice management system, in totals |
| Collected revenue | What the practice actually received in a stated window | Practice financial reports, in totals |

If a stage was not measured, the case study says so rather than estimating it. The reasoning behind separating these stages is set out in our guide to [dental marketing ROI](/blog/dental-marketing-roi/), and the definitions are in the [metrics glossary](/resources/dental-marketing-metrics/).

### Attribution limits and confounders

Marketing data shows association. It rarely proves that one change caused one outcome. Each case study names the factors that could also explain the result, for example a new dentist joining, extended hours, an insurance network change, seasonality, a relocation, or a change to the tracking itself. Where those factors are significant, the case study says the result cannot be attributed to marketing alone.

## What we will never publish

- **Fabricated results**, composites presented as a single real practice, or numbers adjusted to look better.
- **Unpermitted results**: nothing from a practice that has not approved the specific content.
- **Testimonials or reviews that are not genuine.** Federal rules prohibit businesses from creating or knowingly disseminating reviews or testimonials that misrepresent that the reviewer exists, used the service, or what their experience was.
- **Patient information** of any kind.
- **Results from any other business or brand**, presented as ours.
- **Headline percentages without the underlying counts, dates and definitions.**
