Alpha Dental Media works with dental practices, dental groups and DSOs, never with patients directly. We are based in Houston and work remotely with practices nationwide, with priority markets in Texas, Florida, Georgia and Greater Los Angeles.

Our [nine services](/services/) are the same building blocks for every client. What differs is the mix, the order and how success is measured, because a pediatric office, an orthodontic practice and a ten-office group are different businesses even when they compete for similar searches.

## Why practice type changes the marketing mix

Four features of the business decide most marketing choices:

- **Who decides.** A parent choosing a pediatric dentist, an adult comparing implant consultations and a general dentist deciding where to refer a patient are different audiences with different questions.
- **How long the decision takes.** Emergency and routine visits are decided in minutes or days. Implant, full-arch and orthodontic treatment can take weeks or months, so follow-up and measurement windows change.
- **Where patients come from.** Some practices depend on referrals from other dentists or physicians; others depend almost entirely on direct search and reviews.
- **What the schedule can absorb.** Hygiene staffing, consultation time, after-school slots and surgical capacity cap what any campaign can produce.

Capacity is worth stressing. In the ADA Health Policy Institute's Q3 2026 panel of 704 private-practice dentists, 38% reported being not busy enough, while about one-third were recruiting hygienists or assistants and many found it hard. Marketing aimed at the wrong part of the schedule produces longer waits, not more revenue.

<SourceNote ids={["ada-hpi-dental-economy-q3-2026"]}>National self-reported panel.</SourceNote>

The market structure also differs by practice type. In HPI's 2025 data, 17.0% of U.S. dentists were affiliated with a dental support organization, including 16.3% of general practitioners, 16.6% of pediatric dentists and 24.4% of orthodontists. The share in a one-location, one-dentist practice ranged from 21.1% of pediatric dentists to 36.6% of orthodontists.

## Practices that fit more than one row

Many practices sit across categories. A general practice may place implants and run a sizeable cosmetic schedule; a group may include pediatric and orthodontic offices; a startup may be a new office of an established group. In those cases we plan each service line or office on its own terms, then combine them under one set of accounts, tracking standards and reporting, so results can be compared without forcing different businesses into one template.
