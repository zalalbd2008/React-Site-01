These resources are for people deciding how to spend on dental marketing and how to tell whether it worked. None of them requires an account or sends your inputs anywhere, and none is a substitute for your own data: each one is designed to be used with your practice's own numbers.

## Which resource to start with

- **If your reports do not agree with what the front desk sees**, start with the [dental marketing metrics glossary](/resources/dental-marketing-metrics/). It defines each number from impression to collected revenue, says where the data comes from, and explains the most common misreadings — such as treating ad-platform conversions as new patients.
- **If you are planning or reviewing an ad budget**, use the [dental growth calculator](/resources/dental-growth-calculator/). It turns spend, cost per click and your own conversion rates into estimated attended new-patient visits and the cost of each, with fees kept separate from ad spend.
- **If you suspect the website is losing patients**, work through the [dental website audit checklist](/resources/dental-website-audit-checklist/): eight sections, from findability to measurement, each item paired with how to check it.
- **If you are comparing agency proposals**, score them with the [dental agency comparison scorecard](/resources/dental-agency-comparison-scorecard/) on account ownership, fees, reporting, contracts and offboarding.

The [research methodology](/resources/research-methodology/) page explains how we select, date and limit any observations of websites or AI answers described on this site.

## How these tools fit together

The glossary supplies the definitions, the calculator applies them to a budget, the checklist finds where the website loses inquiries, and the scorecard helps you choose who fixes it. Used in that order, they give you a written baseline before you change anything — which is what makes it possible to say later whether a change worked.

If you would rather have the whole path reviewed for you — visibility, website conversion, advertising and measurement — the [Dental Growth Audit](/dental-marketing-audit/) uses the same structure and tells you what we found, the order we would tackle it in, and what we would leave alone.
