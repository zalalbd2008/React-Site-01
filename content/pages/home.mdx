## Dental marketing built around booked, attended visits

Alpha Dental Media is a dental marketing agency based in Houston, Texas, working with dental practices, dental groups and DSOs across the United States. We handle the parts of marketing that decide whether a person searching for a dentist ends up in your chair: how the practice appears in Google Search and Maps, how paid search campaigns are set up and spent, how the website handles a visitor on a phone, and how all of it is measured.

The proposition is narrow on purpose. We are not trying to increase traffic, impressions or calls for their own sake. A marketing program is only useful if it produces patients you want, at a cost you can sustain, in the appointment slots you actually have. So every recommendation starts from three questions about your practice:

1. **Which patients and services do you want more of?** A practice with an open hygiene schedule and one that wants more implant consults need different keywords, pages and budgets.
2. **What can the practice handle?** New-patient capacity, phone coverage at lunch and after hours, and how quickly a form gets answered all cap what marketing can deliver.
3. **What can be measured today?** If nobody can tell an ad call from a recall reminder, the first job is fixing measurement, not spending more.

Answering those honestly sometimes means recommending less work, not more. If the schedule is already full, the first recommendation is about capacity, not a new campaign.

## Who we help

We work with the businesses that buy dental marketing, not with patients. The people we write for are practice owners, dentists comparing agencies, practice managers who run the day-to-day, and the marketing or operations leads at multi-location groups.

Each kind of practice has a different marketing problem:

- **[General and family dentists](/who-we-help/general-dentists/)** usually depend on steady local demand, insurance questions and a reliable hygiene pipeline. Local visibility and an easy booking path matter more than clever campaigns.
- **[Cosmetic and implant practices](/who-we-help/cosmetic-and-implant-practices/)** sell high-consideration treatment, so the work is about consult quality, financing questions and pages that answer cost honestly.
- **[Orthodontists](/who-we-help/orthodontists/)** often market to parents as well as adult patients and rely on referral relationships alongside search.
- **[Pediatric dentists](/who-we-help/pediatric-dentists/)** are chosen by parents, which changes the questions a website must answer and the reviews people read.
- **[Dental startups](/who-we-help/dental-startups/)** have no history, no reviews and no data yet, so the first months are about building foundations correctly rather than optimizing.
- **[Dental groups and DSOs](/who-we-help/dental-groups-dsos/)** need consistent location data, fair comparisons between offices, and a structure that survives acquisitions and rebrands.

## How SEO, Google Ads, the website and measurement work together

These are usually sold as separate products, often by separate vendors. In practice they are one system, and the weak link sets the result. Ranking well does not help if the booking page fails on a phone. A well-built ad account does not help if calls go to voicemail. Neither can be improved if the reporting counts every call as a new patient.

Several of these responsibilities map onto specific platform controls. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence (how well known a business is), and that businesses with complete, accurate information are more likely to show up in relevant local searches; its Business Profile guidelines require the name and details to reflect the real-world business. In Google Ads, location targeting and ad schedules limit where and when ads can run, negative keywords exclude search terms, and conversion actions can be marked primary (used for bidding) or secondary (observed only). That last setting is how calls, forms and bookings can be tracked separately without letting a weak signal steer the budget. Offline conversion imports let outcomes that happen after the click, such as a booked appointment, be fed back into the account.

Two stages cause most reporting disputes. Inquiries include calls from existing patients, vendors and job applicants, so a count of inquiries alone overstates results; deciding which ones were qualified usually needs the front desk's help or call recordings. And the gap between bookings and attended visits is where marketing value can disappear without anyone noticing. The [dental marketing metrics glossary](/resources/dental-marketing-metrics/) gives the full definition of each stage, where its data comes from and how to calculate cost per attended visit.

<Illustrative title="Why the stage you measure changes the decision">
A practice spends 3,000 dollars a month on ads and the agency report shows 120 calls. That looks like 25 dollars a call. Listening to a sample reveals that 50 of the calls were existing patients, 15 were wrong numbers or sales calls, and 55 were potential new patients. Of those, 30 booked and 24 attended. The real figure is 125 dollars per attended visit, five times the headline. Neither number is good or bad on its own; the point is that a budget decision based on 25 dollars a call would be made on the wrong evidence.
</Illustrative>

### What can be measured about AI search visibility

AI search is a newer channel, so it is worth being precise about what the data shows. Google states that there are no additional requirements or special optimizations for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond normal SEO fundamentals, that a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, and that traffic from these features is included in the Search Console Performance report under the Web search type rather than reported separately. Google also notes that AI Overviews often do not appear, and that the two features may use different models, so the responses and links shown vary. For ChatGPT, OpenAI says sites that opt out of its OAI-SearchBot crawler will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though they can still appear as navigational links, so crawl access is a precondition rather than a ranking lever.

<SourceNote ids={["google-ai-features-search", "openai-crawlers"]} />

In practice that means a practice can check crawl access, keep its public facts consistent, record what selected AI tools say for a fixed set of questions on dated occasions, and watch overall search and referral traffic. It cannot obtain a complete count of AI mentions or a stable "AI ranking". Our [AI search optimization service](/services/dental-ai-search-optimization/) works within those limits.

## How an engagement runs

Work follows the same five stages whatever the service: **diagnose, plan, implement, measure, review.** We start by establishing a baseline: what is visible, what is tracked, and what your practice management system says about new patients. The plan sets out what we would change, in what order, and why, including what we would leave alone. Implementation needs things only the practice can provide, such as account access, content approvals and an accurate picture of capacity. Reporting separates what was done from what changed, and what is measured from what is estimated.

The [detailed walkthrough of our process](/our-process/) covers each stage, the decisions you make along the way and what a report contains.

## Evidence, not testimonials

We do not publish client results, testimonials or logos without the client's written permission, and we will not show you numbers that cannot be checked. When a case study is published it will state the baseline period, the measurement period, where the data came from, which services were involved, and what else changed in the practice that could explain the result. Until then, the [case studies page](/case-studies/) says plainly that none are published yet.

That is a deliberate choice. A headline percentage increase in leads means little without knowing what a lead was, what the starting point was and whether the practice also added a dentist or moved office. When you evaluate any agency, including us, ask for the baseline, the method and the data source behind each result. The [agency comparison scorecard](/resources/dental-agency-comparison-scorecard/) turns those questions into a structured comparison.

What we can show before any engagement is how we think: the audit findings for your own practice, the reasoning behind each recommendation, and the [editorial standards](/editorial-policy/) behind everything we publish.
