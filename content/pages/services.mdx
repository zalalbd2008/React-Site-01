## How to choose among nine dental marketing services

Most practices arrive with a symptom rather than a service in mind: the phone is quieter than last year, a new competitor is showing above them on the map, the ad bill keeps rising, or the website looks dated. The right first service depends on where in the chain from search to attended visit the loss is happening, and a symptom can have more than one cause. Fewer calls might mean lower visibility, a broken tracking number, or calls going unanswered at lunch.

So we sort the decision into three questions before recommending anything:

1. **Can you measure what is happening now?** If calls, forms and bookings are not tracked separately, or nobody can tell a new patient from a recall, measurement comes first. Otherwise every later decision is a guess.
2. **Are people finding you?** If the practice is hard to find for the services and areas it wants, the work is visibility: organic search, the Google Business Profile, or paid search when results are needed sooner.
3. **Do people who find you get in touch and book?** If visibility is reasonable but inquiries are low, or inquiries are high but bookings are low, the problem is the website, the phones or the follow-up, not traffic.

The services below map to those questions. They overlap deliberately, because a practice rarely has only one problem, but each has a clear job.

## How the services fit together

The nine services fall into four groups.

**Measurement.** [Conversion tracking and attribution](/services/dental-conversion-tracking/) is the foundation. It separates calls, forms and bookings, distinguishes new from existing patients where your systems allow, and reconciles marketing data with the schedule. We usually check it first whatever else is planned.

**Visibility.** [Dental SEO](/services/dental-seo-company/) covers the website's technical health, page relevance and local signals. [Local SEO and Google Business Profile](/services/dental-local-seo-google-business-profile/) focuses on a single practice's map presence and listings. [Multi-location dental SEO](/services/local-seo-multi-location-clinics/) applies the same disciplines across a group, where consistency and structure matter most. [Google Ads management](/services/dental-google-ads-management/) buys visibility directly for the services and areas you choose, and its search terms report shows which actual searches triggered the ads (Google omits low-volume terms for privacy, so it never shows everything). [AI search optimization](/services/dental-ai-search-optimization/) looks at how AI assistants and AI search features describe the practice.

**Conversion.** [Website design and conversion](/services/dental-web-design-conversion/) is about whether a visitor on a phone can understand the offer and act. [Content marketing](/services/dental-content-marketing/) produces the treatment, location and practice pages that answer patients' real questions; any clinical statements are reviewed by your own clinicians.

**Trust.** [Reputation management](/services/dental-reputation-management/) builds a request process that does not rely on incentives or filtering out unhappy patients, and prepares responses that never disclose patient information.

## Do you need SEO and Google Ads at the same time?

Not necessarily. Paid search can produce inquiries soon after launch but stops when spend stops; SEO takes longer to show results but keeps working without a per-click cost. Running both makes sense when you need inquiries now and want to reduce reliance on ads later, or when ad data can show which services and messages are worth building pages for. If budget allows only one, the choice depends on how quickly you need results, how competitive your market is and how ready the website is.

## When a website rebuild is, and is not, the answer

A rebuild is the right first step when the site cannot be fixed in place: it fails on phones, cannot be edited, is slow for structural reasons, or the practice is rebranding. It is the wrong first step when the real problem is a broken form, an unanswered phone or a missing Business Profile. Rebuilds also carry migration risk to existing visibility, which must be planned for.
