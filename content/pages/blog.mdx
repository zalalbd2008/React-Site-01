These guides are written for the people who make marketing decisions in a dental practice: owners, practice managers, and the operations and marketing leads in dental groups. They are not patient information and they do not give clinical advice. Each one takes a single question, answers it directly, and then explains the tradeoffs, what the practice has to do itself, and how to tell whether the work is paying off.

Where a guide relies on platform rules, it cites the current official documentation from Google or the relevant authority and states the date we checked it. Where a guide uses numbers to explain a calculation, they are marked as illustrative. Read our [editorial policy](/editorial-policy/) for how guides are researched and updated.

## The six topic areas

**SEO and local visibility.** Why a website does not rank, how long SEO takes, what it should cost, and why a practice may not appear in Google Maps. Includes Google Business Profile eligibility, practitioner listings and suspensions.

**Advertising.** How to structure Google Ads for dental services: match types, negative keywords, emergency campaigns that respect opening hours, implant advertising, and how to size a budget from the appointments you can actually offer.

**Websites and conversion.** Why visitors leave without booking, how to audit the mobile booking path, the choice between online booking and request forms, which website platform suits a practice, and how to measure calls without counting every call as a new patient.

**Dental groups.** Site structure for multiple locations, location pages that are not near-duplicates, keeping visibility through acquisitions and rebrands, and comparing offices fairly in a group dashboard.

**AI search.** What AI-generated answers mean for a practice, how to check whether they describe your practice accurately, how structured data should match the real business, and what AI referral data can and cannot show.

**Reputation and strategy.** Requesting reviews without incentives or gating, responding to negative reviews without disclosing patient information, the first 90 days of a new practice, choosing an agency, and measuring return from lead to collected revenue.
