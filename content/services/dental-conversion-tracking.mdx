## The measurement problem, and our direct recommendation

Most dental marketing reports measure activity and call it success. A "conversion" in Google Ads might be a click on the header phone number from an existing patient. A "lead" from a call-tracking tool might be a 12-second wrong number. A "new patient" in a monthly report might be estimated rather than counted. When those figures feed budget decisions and automated bidding, the practice spends more on whatever is easiest to count.

Our recommendation is to define four stages before buying any tool: an **inquiry** (a real attempt to contact the practice about care), a **qualified inquiry** (a prospective patient for a service you offer, in your area), a **booked appointment** (confirmed in the scheduling system) and an **attended visit** (the patient came in). Marketing platforms can measure the first stage. Only the practice can measure the last two. Attribution is the work of joining them without moving patient information into marketing systems.

For how these stages relate to revenue and profit, see [telling leads, bookings, visits and revenue apart](/blog/dental-marketing-roi/); the [metrics glossary](/resources/dental-marketing-metrics/) holds the definitions we use.

## How we audit current tracking

### What is being counted, and where

We list every conversion action in Google Ads, every key event in Google Analytics 4 and every source of "leads" in other tools. Google now uses "key event" in Analytics for an action important to the business, and reserves "conversion" for actions used to measure ad campaigns and optimise bidding. In Google Ads, primary conversion actions are used for bidding and reporting while secondary actions are observed only. Audits often find page views, scroll depth and duplicate form tags marked as primary.

<SourceNote ids={["ga4-key-events-vs-conversions", "google-ads-primary-secondary-conversions"]} />

### Whether calls are measured or merely counted

Calls are where most dental inquiries happen and where measurement is weakest. Google's call reporting uses Google forwarding numbers on ads to record call duration, start time, caller number and whether the call connected, and lets advertisers count calls above a set duration as conversions. Google can also classify calls by analysing recordings where recording is enabled in the US and Canada. A duration threshold filters out wrong numbers but still counts existing-patient calls, so we look at what share of qualifying calls the front desk would recognise as new-patient inquiries. The method is explained in [measuring dental calls without counting every call as a new patient](/blog/dental-call-tracking/).

<SourceNote ids={["google-ads-call-reporting", "google-ads-measure-calls"]} />

### Whether forms and bookings confirm

We submit every form and attempt every online booking on a phone, then check what fires: a tag on button click (which counts failed submissions), a tag on a thank-you page (which counts reloads) or a confirmed server response. We also check that the practice actually receives the submission and that someone follows it up.

### Whether anything leaks into analytics that should not

Google's own policies prohibit sending personally identifiable information such as email addresses or phone numbers to Analytics, and Google notes that it often leaks through page URLs and titles. On dental sites, booking confirmation URLs, form query strings and appointment page titles can carry names, phone numbers or the treatment requested. We crawl the site and review the data streams for this.

## Privacy and consent: what limits dental tracking

This is the part of the work where we are most careful, and where the practice's own counsel has the final word. We describe the landscape; we do not certify any configuration as compliant.

**Is the practice a HIPAA covered entity?** HIPAA's definition of a covered entity includes a health care provider that transmits health information electronically in connection with a covered transaction — which describes most practices that bill dental insurance electronically. Your counsel should confirm your status, because it shapes everything below.

<SourceNote ids={["ecfr-45-cfr-160-103"]} />

**The HHS tracking-technologies bulletin and the 2024 ruling.** HHS's Office for Civil Rights issued a bulletin in December 2022, updated in March 2024, on covered entities' use of tracking technologies such as cookies and pixels on websites, patient portals and apps. On June 20, 2024, the US District Court for the Northern District of Texas, in *American Hospital Association v. Becerra*, declared unlawful and vacated one part of the March 2024 bulletin: the position that an IP address combined with a visit to an unauthenticated public webpage addressing specific health conditions or health care providers is individually identifiable health information. HHS filed an appeal and then withdrew it in August 2024. Legal commentary at the time noted that the rest of the bulletin — including its treatment of authenticated pages such as patient portals — remained as subregulatory guidance.

<SourceNote ids={["aha-v-becerra-opinion-2024", "aha-news-hhs-no-appeal-2024", "nixon-peabody-ocr-tracking-2024"]} />

We could not retrieve the current HHS bulletin page while preparing this page, so we do not describe its present wording. In practice, the ruling narrowed one scenario; it did not make every marketing tag on a dental website risk-free. Pages behind a login, appointment request forms, booking flows and anything that pairs an identifier with a treatment or condition still need careful review. State privacy and wiretap laws are separate from HIPAA and can apply to the same tags.

**Consent banners and consent mode.** Google's consent mode passes a visitor's choices from a consent banner to Google tags so they adjust their behaviour; it does not provide the banner, and it is not in itself a HIPAA safeguard. We configure it where the practice uses a consent platform, but treat it as one layer, not the answer.

<SourceNote ids={["ga4-consent-mode"]} />

**The FTC Health Breach Notification Rule.** The FTC rule applies to vendors of personal health records and related entities, and states that it does not apply to HIPAA-covered entities or to entities acting as their business associates. It is mostly relevant if a practice or group runs consumer health apps or tools outside its HIPAA activities — another question for counsel.

<SourceNote ids={["ecfr-16-cfr-318"]} />

**Call recording.** Recording calls to judge inquiry quality brings in state consent law as well as HIPAA. California, for example, generally makes it an offence to record a confidential telephone communication without the consent of all parties. We default to duration thresholds and front-desk outcome codes, and only enable recording-based classification after legal review.

<SourceNote ids={["ca-penal-code-632"]} />

## What the service delivers

- **A measurement plan** naming each stage, its definition, its source system and its owner.
- **Clean conversion actions** in Google Ads and key events in Analytics, with primary actions limited to genuine inquiries.
- **Call measurement** using ad call reporting, duration thresholds and, where useful, website call tracking that does not disturb listing consistency.
- **Form and booking events** that fire only on confirmed submissions, with no personal data in URLs or event parameters.
- **A front-desk recording standard**: a short referral-source list, outcome codes for inquiries (new patient booked, existing patient, not a fit, no answer) and a rule for when to enter them.
- **Offline outcome imports, where appropriate.** Google Ads supports importing offline conversions using the Google click identifier saved with an inquiry, so bidding can learn from booked or attended appointments. Google now recommends enhanced conversions for leads, which matches imported outcomes using hashed data such as email addresses, and notes that from June 15, 2026 offline conversion and enhanced-conversions-for-leads uploads move to the Data Manager API. Sending any patient-derived data to Google — even hashed — is a decision for the practice and its counsel; where it is not approved, we import counts or use click identifiers only, or report outcomes outside the ad platform.
- **A monthly report** that separates the four stages by channel and office, with a change log.

## Dependencies and owner responsibilities

Attribution fails most often at the front desk, not in the tag manager. If referral source is skipped when the phone is busy, or entered as "Google" for every patient, no software can recover it. We keep the recording burden small — one field, a short list, one outcome code — and we ask the practice manager to own its consistency.

There are three common levels of scope, and we do not publish prices for any of them:

| Option | What it covers | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Clean-up | Conversion definitions, call reporting, form events, PII review | Still stops at inquiries; bookings remain estimated |
| Stage reporting | Clean-up plus front-desk outcome codes and monthly summary exports | Depends on consistent staff entry; manual reporting effort |
| Offline outcomes | Stage reporting plus approved imports of booked or attended outcomes into Google Ads | Needs counsel approval and a reliable export from the practice system |

Effort grows with the number of offices, phone systems and booking tools; whether a central call team exists; how many platforms need connecting; and how much legal review the practice requires. ## Worked example: one month, measured two ways

<Illustrative title="A single-office general practice before and after a tracking clean-up">
**Before.** The practice's agency report shows 140 "leads" in a month: 85 Google Ads conversions, 40 call-tracking calls and 15 forms. The practice management system shows 31 new patients booked, but the referral source field is blank for 20 of them.

**What the audit finds.** Ads count every call of any length and every click on the header phone number. Call tracking includes existing patients and calls under 20 seconds. Two form tags fire on the same submission. The booking confirmation URL contains the patient's first name.

**After.** Primary ad conversions are limited to ad calls over the agreed duration, confirmed forms and completed bookings. The front desk records source and outcome for every new-patient inquiry. The name is removed from the confirmation URL and the tag configuration goes to the practice's counsel for review.

**The next month's report.** 58 inquiries, of which 41 are qualified (new prospective patients for services offered). 29 are booked and 24 attend. Ads account for 14 of the attended visits, organic search and maps for 7, referrals for 3. The headline number is smaller, but each figure now means one thing, and the practice can compare channel cost with attended visits rather than with leads.
</Illustrative>
